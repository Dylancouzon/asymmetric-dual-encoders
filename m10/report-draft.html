Nano Retry Plan

M9 built nano, a 33.4M-parameter query encoder distilled into stella's space, and it kept 82% of the teacher on dev. The number hides two findings: 94% where the training queries look like the test queries and 50 to 71% where they do not, and a 384-wide linear head that a closed-form probe shows binding under L2 regression before training began. M10 fixes both, tests both, and keeps every guardrail that makes the eventual number believable.

What M9 found

The build ran the locked recipe: bge-small regressed by plain L2 onto stella-400M's query vectors, 5% queries / 5% spans / 90% documents, constant learning rate, plateau rule. It stopped at a third of the registered dose because the dev curve had been flat for 1.1B tokens. The anneal added +0.004. The candidate is frozen and not released.

A 33.4M-parameter student that reaches 94% on one distribution has the capacity to reach 94%. The 463K training queries were all Wikipedia QA or product search, the longest 108 words. The six test sets are scientific claims, biomedical and finance questions, 170-word counter-arguments, paper titles and COVID queries, none of which the pool contains. The macro on the six is therefore expected below 82%, not above it.

The second cause: a 384-wide head is at the ceiling

A closed-form probe ran on the Mac while this plan was written. It takes stella's own query vectors, keeps only their top-k principal directions, and retrieves against stella's unmodified document vectors of the two forum components. A student with a 384-wide hidden state and a linear head emits vectors inside one 384-dimensional subspace, and the subspace that plain L2 regression pushes it toward is the reconstruction-optimal one that PCA computes. The k = 384 rows are that subspace's retention. They are evidence about the class under L2 distillation, not a bound over every possible subspace.

One 384-dimensional subspace serves one query distribution almost perfectly and several at once poorly, and M9's 50 to 71% on the same components sits below these rows. The aim needs 89.7% across six distributions, so under L2 regression the M9 head width bound it before training started; coverage alone would not have fixed it. At 640 dimensions the same subspace retains 98 to 100%. Within 35M parameters the width comes from the feature, not the backbone: mean-pool three layers and concatenate them into a 1152-wide feature before the linear head. That adds 0.79M parameters, stays linear, and still exports per token so fastembed's own mean pooling reproduces it exactly. The Mac also reproduced the training box's teacher scores on both components to four decimals. Two follow-ups ran the same afternoon: a frozen bge-small with a closed-form head retains 27, 33 and 37 percent of stella on programmers as the feature widens from one to three pooled layers (36, 41 and 44 on physics), and fastembed 0.8.0 serves the three-layer per-token head with its own mean pooling to within 2e-7 of the training-time form, so the wider head costs nothing at serving time.

The thesis is tested before anything is built

Eleven screen arms at 5% of the build dose, in seven decision families and thirteen contrasts. Family A holds everything fixed and varies only the data: the M9 pool, the pool plus enough PAQ factoid questions to match the full corpus's volume, and the full corpus with its 12 forms. Three registered outcomes on the forms-versus-volume contrast: a corrected lower bound above the 0.0056 margin resolves the coverage thesis on the COV families; a positive but smaller bound lets the build proceed with the verdict labelled unresolved; anything else stops M10 before any build and returns it to Dylan with all three rows. The other families decide mix (100/0, 75/25, 50/50 at matched query presentations), init, phase 2, batch (32 vs 128 at equal examples), student, and output width (384, 768, 1152), each with a default and a Bonferroni-corrected bound over the thirteen contrasts. The student tie goes to MiniLM-L6 (23.9M with the head) and bge-small (34.5M) must win outright, because 33M already sits at the top of what counts as low compute. Any decision that went against its default is re-trained with two more seeds for both the winner and the default; a margin inside the seed range reverts. The selected recipe is then trained once as a whole and checked against the anchor on LoTTE, a surface no decision has touched, under M9's non-inferiority rule.

Selection happens on COV, a new licensed, decontaminated, qrel-bearing surface made only of components no M10 decision has read, weighted equally across families: consumer health (MedicalQARetrieval), BRIGHT (six technical and economics slices as one family), legal (one LegalBench task; the other is non-commercial and refused), and finance (an annual-report QA set, pending checks). Licences were checked at their primary sources; Climate-FEVER was refused again for having none. The two CQADupStack dev components were scored by today's Mac diagnostics, so they stay in the secondary dev suite and out of the selector. Every admitted corpus joins the protected index before any training text is built. At least three families must survive. COV has no scientific-claim, paper-title or argument retrieval, so the coverage verdict is a verdict about the COV families, and those three forms are tested only by the six-set transaction.

Stages

  1. M10.0 diagnosis and screen lock. The three Mac diagnostics are done (rank probe, head-width probe, serving parity). On the box: the M9 candidate's per-component dev read, COV admission and its addition to the protected index, then the pushed screen lock. The 35M cap is hard, so the 109M capacity probe is optional and report-only. No six-set, reserved or LoTTE output exists during this stage.
  2. M10.1 data. Per-form 200-query smoke read by a person and rate measured, then generation at scale, PAQ sample, decontamination, held-out forms, teacher targets, hard-candidate mining, manifest.
  3. M10.2 screen and recipe lock. The arms, the confirmation seeds, one pushed lock with every field filled, reviewed by Codex and Fable. Only then: M9's close-out on its frozen candidate, amended to six-only so it cannot spend the reserved access, and LoTTE read #1 as a veto.
  4. M10.3 build. 50M examples, extension by one cycle if the last annealed checkpoint still gains, at most two. Export, parity, freeze, pre-freeze review, LoTTE read #2.
  5. M10.4 final. The single six-set transaction, then the reserved batch if either contrast passes.

Unimpeachable by competitors

  • Comparators byte-verified against official artifacts, scored in one exact-search harness, frozen as per-query vectors before any M10 number existed. The bridge check proves the harness unchanged.
  • Pre-registration with pushed commits as the external witness. One six-set transaction per milestone, each disclosed. The reserved four's single access. Dev-reuse count and the selection-surface licence table published.
  • Contamination handled three ways: stella's disclosed overlap on ArguAna and FiQA at every headline, the no-disclosed-overlap rows, and reserved and LoTTE surfaces no decision touched.
  • Training data affirmatively licensed, no MS MARCO, attribution recorded. Synthetic queries with a permissive generator, per-query provenance and removal counts per screen.
  • Compute disclosed in examples, tokens and GPU-hours beside LEAF's ~100 A100-hours.
  • System-level framing only. The comparison is stella documents plus nano queries against arctic documents plus LEAF queries. No statement isolates nano from its document tower.
  • Reproducibility: code, corpus manifests with hashes, model revisions, seeds, tested statistics code, and the full screen table including losing arms.

Review record and where things live

Six Codex passes (gpt-5.6-terra, read-only, high effort) reviewed the plan, each on the text the previous one had changed. Pass 1 returned 3 blockers, 10 majors and 2 minors: the M9 close-out moved behind M10's lock, reserved-set document fingerprints were dropped along with FineWeb documents, the coverage surface replaced the two CQA components as selector, and the screen gained its data-only family, mix family and multiplicity control. Pass 2 confirmed ten fixes, rejected five, and added 3 blockers, 8 majors and 1 minor: COV joined the protected index, M9's close-out became six-only, family B was redesigned around matched query presentations, family A gained its volume control, and the cost arithmetic was redone. Pass 3 confirmed twelve and left five gaps: the coverage rule's three outcomes, the volume arm's exact size, an executable LoTTE veto, the four-family floor in the status file, and the confirmation budget. Pass 4 confirmed those, checked the probe's algebra and the width family's parameter count, stopped the probe's result from being called a bound, fixed the coverage rule's remaining contradiction, and corrected five numbers on this page. Pass 5 read the whole set for contradictions and omissions: it moved the CQADupStack pair out of the selector because the Mac diagnostics had read it, forced the two data arms to identical sizes, wrote the generation contract (200 queries per form, pinned artifacts, a pass rule, Dylan as approver), and produced the ledger skeleton the box fills in. Pass 6 verified those fixes and left two recorded dissents for Dylan to rule on: whether BRIGHT's third-party documents meet the evaluation licence standard (the plan applies the same dataset-level standard that admitted the six), and whether the coverage surface needs four families rather than three. Every finding and disposition is in m10/PLANNING.md §8. All six logs were audited for reserved-set reads: clean.
