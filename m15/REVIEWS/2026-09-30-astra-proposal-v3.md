# Codex gpt-6-astra, read-only, adversarial review of the v3 proposal

Date 2026-09-30. Access: audited: named result files only, no work/, no protected paths. Returned text below, verbatim. Dispositions are in `m15/LOG.md` (2026-09-30) and `m15/PLAN.md`.

---

1. **P1 — The training-contact claim omits inherited exposure.** “Nano, trained with no MS MARCO in any role” (`proposal.md:9`) is too broad. Nano uses pretrained bge-small (`results/m13_build_record.json:119`; `m10src/nano10.py:57,88`), whose MS MARCO training is explicitly acknowledged in `research/m7-data-licensing.md:36`. This is the strongest rejection risk: the proposed symmetric contact analysis overlooks the student’s initialization while counting comparator training. **Minimal fix:** say “no MS MARCO supplied to our distillation pipeline”; disclose backbone initialization and teacher exposure separately from direct training inputs, for every system.

2. **P1 — The contact partitions are numerically correct but cannot establish a cleaner generalization result.** The quoted 10-set and 7-set macros reproduce, but the seven retain source-family contact through Touché and development contact within CQADupStack (`m20/beir15_registry.json:492–500`; `m20/REGISTRATION.md:102–109`). Nano’s listed sources are also incomplete: the build includes ESCI, Mr. TyDi, SQuAD and TriviaQA (`results/m13_build_record.json:597–603`). Claim-form rows describe linguistic form, not dataset provenance (`results/m13_build_record.json:679–686`). **Minimal fix:** publish a per-system contact matrix covering direct training, inherited weights, teacher disclosure, development use and unknowns; use identical dataset membership across systems. Keep these as explicitly post-hoc descriptive sensitivities. The macros themselves are not significance claims; presenting their ranking as recovered superiority would violate `m20/beir15_registry.json:543–546`.

3. **P1 — The “fusion rule” is unsupported by its proposed evidence.** The correlations reproduce, but both axes subtract the same dense score: `fusion − dense` versus `BM25 − dense` (`proposal.md:37,62`). Shared subtraction can generate correlation; moreover, the predictor requires relevance labels, and the 30 points are paired observations from 15 datasets. This does not establish a prospective per-corpus decision rule. **Minimal fix:** retain a descriptive association, disclose the mathematical coupling and pairing, and remove the actionable rule claim. Do not select a policy from reserved rows (`CLAUDE.md:25–28`; `m20/beir15_registry.json:546`).

4. **P1 — E2 does not yet measure the advertised eight-system frontier.** E1/E2 omit lexical retrieval and fusion execution costs despite including both fused systems and BM25 in F1 (`proposal.md:35,60,75–76`). They also say “every query encoder” swaps against Stella, although bge-small and LEAF require their own document spaces (`proposal.md:12`). **Minimal fix:** restrict the shared-index demonstration to Zero, Nano and Stella; measure external systems against their respective indexes. Either include BM25 execution, DBSF@100 and lexical-index storage in system costs, or omit fused/BM25 frontier points. Record unchanged collection identity/content across swaps; returned IDs alone do not establish that the index remained unchanged.

5. **P1 — E2 needs a narrower, interpretable deployment estimand.** Recall@10 against exact search on the same 1M subset measures ANN neighbor recovery, not relevance retention or BEIR-15 quality (`proposal.md:76`; `CLAUDE.md:52–53`). Combining that latency with full-corpus exact quality can look like one measured operating point. Memory limits also lack a stated boundary: server-only limits exclude client encoders and potentially substantial memory outside server RSS. E2 gives no tail-latency sample count; E1’s 20 samples cannot support a credible p99. **Minimal fix:** pin a non-reserved corpus/subset, common queries, per-encoder exact references, search settings, workload and memory accounting. Label F1’s quality and latency as separate workloads. Use adequate samples or omit p99. A single deployment configuration plus one quantized alternative suffices initially; the full quantization-by-memory sweep is unnecessary to demonstrate index reuse.

6. **P1 — E3/E5 cannot diagnose “single-prompt inheritance.”** Cosine-to-teacher correlation cannot distinguish teacher weakness, student approximation error and ranking sensitivity. Changing only Stella’s prompt cannot show how a student trained against different targets would behave (`proposal.md:54,77–79`). The single-prompt denominator is defensible because it is frozen in `m20/REGISTRATION.md:59–71`; it measures retention of that specific path. However, the external gap is not a prompt-only experiment: precision, length and potentially output dimension differ, and the author explicitly qualified their recollection in [discussion 21](https://huggingface.co/NovaSearch/stella_en_400M_v5/discussions/21). **Minimal fix:** keep the denominator qualified everywhere; describe E3 as alignment association and E5 as teacher prompt sensitivity. Leave the student failure undiagnosed. Pin the historical source for 58.97 and the dataset deltas; the current card did not expose that number in the retrieved text.

7. **P2 — Two provenance statements need correction.** The M9 exclusion is justified, but “untrained MiniLM” is inaccurate: its backbone is pretrained and its projection head randomly initialized (`m9src/edge_cost.py:88–90`); the synthetic index/table are confirmed by `bench/edge_prototype_pair.py:6–11`. Also, the claimed new-tower build cost omits reselection and validation explicitly required alongside the 20-minute retraining estimate (`proposal.md:90`; `m7/LEDGER.md:225–228`). **Minimal fix:** identify the prototype as pretrained MiniLM with an untrained head; label the quoted build figures as component costs rather than complete new-tower costs.

**Verdict: Must fix before drafting; the core numerical results and headline/held-out contrasts check out, but inherited-contact disclosure and unsupported deployment/diagnostic claims undermine the proposed interpretation.**
