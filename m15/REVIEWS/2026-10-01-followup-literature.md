# Follow-up literature and experiment recommendation (2026-10-01)

Scope: Constella `m15/PAPER.md` v12, E2 method in `m15/MEASUREMENTS.md`, public E2 summary in `m15/EVIDENCE.md`, and explicit E2 receipt `results/m15_e2_ann_msmarco1m.json`. This is a focused check of whether the shared-index ANN penalty could support a stronger research finding. No experiment, rental, or paper edit was made.

## What is already observed

On the positive-preserving 1M MS MARCO diagnostic, with the same Stella document vectors and nominal HNSW parameters, unquantized `ef=16` loses 16.3%, 7.0%, and 4.4% of each tier's own exact nDCG@10 for Zero, Nano, and Stella. Exact-neighbor recovery@10 is 82.4%, 91.0%, and 93.1%. Zero's median nearest-document cosine is 0.60 versus Nano's 0.74 and Stella's 0.78. These facts establish a **query-encoder-dependent ANN penalty** on this measured setup; they do not establish that distance to the document manifold causes the penalty. Exact-search ranking margins, local neighbor density/connectivity, and relevance distribution may also differ.

The existing registered sweep already suggests a quantization interaction at matched `ef=64`: recovery@10 changes from unquantized to 1-bit binary (with full-vector rescoring, oversampling 1) by **−5.53 percentage points** for Zero (0.9377→0.8824), **−2.73** for Nano (0.9736→0.9463), and **−1.77** for Stella (0.9800→0.9623). At `ef=16`, the drops are −6.14, −3.78, and −2.79 points. This is a descriptive aggregate comparison across separately configured quantized and unquantized collections; it does not yet isolate quantized distance scoring from graph construction or other collection differences. The new 1-bit result should begin by reusing these rows, not by repeating the sweep.

## Seven primary sources and novelty boundary

1. [OOD-DiskANN (Jaiswal et al., 2022/WWW 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12850) directly establishes that data-dependent ANN indexes can cost much more for out-of-distribution (OOD) queries than in-distribution queries; it uses sample OOD queries at index construction to improve the graph. **Consequence:** “OOD queries are hard for ANN” is prior art. Constella's distinctive condition is the *same pretrained text document graph* queried by several compatible encoders, with no rebuild.
2. [RoarGraph (Chen et al., PVLDB 2024)](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol17/p2735-chen.pdf) finds that cross-modal queries are farther from indexed vectors and their true neighbors are more spatially dispersed; its remedy builds a query-aware projected bipartite graph. **Consequence:** nearest-document cosine alone is a weak explanation; examine spacing/graph connectivity among each query's exact top-k documents. Its index intervention violates Constella's shared-index constraint, which is a useful comparison, not a failure of their method.
3. [Aumüller and Ceccarello, Information Systems 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306437921000569) shows local intrinsic dimensionality and relative contrast can select ANN queries of different difficulty; workload averages hide this. **Consequence:** measure per-query effort/recovery, stratify by local geometry, and do not equate a lower top-1 cosine with causal difficulty.
4. [Steiner-Hardness (Wang et al., PVLDB 2024)](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol17/p4668-wang.pdf) argues distance-only hardness proxies can miss graph structure and proposes a graph-native connection measure that better tracks effort. **Consequence:** a serious mechanism study should add at least one graph-neighborhood or exact-top-k connectivity diagnostic; merely regressing failure on cosine is not enough.
5. [Ada-ef (Distribution-Aware Exploration for Adaptive HNSW Search, 2025 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06636v1) predicts per-query exploration from query/database similarity distributions and reports lower latency at a target recall. **Consequence:** per-query `ef` as such is prior art. A Constella contribution would need to show an encoder-swap-specific, fixed-index rule outperforming this kind of geometry-only baseline, or present adaptive `ef` as an engineering application rather than a new algorithm.
6. [Qdrant quantization documentation](https://qdrant.tech/documentation/manage-data/quantization/) describes 1-bit sensitivity to component distributions, `ignore` (use original vectors), `rescore` (rerank selected candidates with original vectors), and `oversampling` (widen candidate selection before reranking). It also documents asymmetric quantization that preserves query magnitudes against binary stored vectors. **Consequence:** a quantization study can separate candidate selection from final ordering on one collection, but verify those toggles against the tested Qdrant 1.19.1 binary; the current web documentation may describe newer defaults.
7. [QA-Cos (Nyang, ICML 2026)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/nyang26a.html) improves binary-sketch similarity estimation and candidate selection, including an HNSW scorer replacement. **Consequence:** query-aware correction of binary search is established; “binary quantization hurts some queries, fix the scorer” cannot be a broad priority claim. The potential Constella result is whether a *query encoder's representational shift* predictably amplifies that loss over a fixed document index.

An additional recent [SHEAF preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12229) estimates query-specific graph difficulty from changes in shallow top-k probe results. It further narrows any claim that adaptive search based on a cheap probe is new. I did not independently reproduce its reported gains.

## Ranked bounded experiments

### 1. Preferred: decompose the fixed-index ANN tax

**Decision it answers:** Does replacing only the query encoder create an ANN penalty beyond each encoder's exact retrieval loss, and is the extra penalty mediated by quantized candidate generation or by traversal geometry already present in full precision?

Use one existing 1M document collection/graph at a time and identical query IDs for Zero, Nano, and Stella. At a small fixed grid of `ef` (for example 16, 64, 256), compare exact top-10 to (a) unquantized HNSW, (b) on a *single* 1-bit collection with quantization `ignore=true`, (c) quantized search with rescoring disabled, and (d) quantized search with original-vector rescoring at oversampling 1 and 4. Reuse the E2 point IDs/vector receipts; first confirm Qdrant 1.19.1 implements the toggles as documented. Report per-query exact-neighbor recovery and nDCG delta to each tier's **own exact result**, plus search p50/p95. Raw nDCG across tiers answers a different question. Compare per-query tier differences and confidence intervals by paired query resampling; do not call this confirmatory unless registered before new observation.

The same-collection `ignore` versus quantized comparison is especially valuable because the document set and graph remain fixed. `rescore=false` versus `rescore=true` isolates ordering **within returned candidates** only if candidate generation is otherwise identical; validate that behavior for the installed Qdrant version. Oversampling tests whether widening the candidate pool repairs a selection failure. Do not describe `rescore=true` at oversampling 1 as fixing missed neighbors: the exact neighbor has to enter the candidate set first.

For mechanism, derive per-query exact top-k spacing/margin, local relative contrast or LID, top-k document-document distances, and (if a graph export is practical) connectivity of those exact neighbors. Match or stratify the three encoders by exact-result difficulty so that a mere difference in exact rankings does not masquerade as an ANN-specific effect. A controlled query-side calibration is warranted only if the residual gap persists. Try one cheap frozen-index correction, selected on development queries and tested on held-out queries: a low-rank/diagonal affine map or centroid/covariance correction applied to Zero vectors before normalization. Its success criterion is **lower search work at matched exact nDCG and matched ANN recovery**, including the transform's CPU time and size; a quality-changing transform that simply moves queries toward Stella is not evidence of improved navigability.

**Stop condition:** If matched exact-neighbor difficulty accounts for the tier gap, or no correction improves the latency/quality frontier beyond retuning `ef` and oversampling, report that as the substantive finding. Do not build a new graph or train a full query model for this question.

### 2. Secondary: encoder-aware `ef` allocation

Using the same data, test whether a cheap pre-search Zero signal (for example query-document distance statistics from a tiny sampled centroid set, or a shallow probe) predicts the minimum `ef` needed for the query's own exact-neighbor recovery. Compare with a global `ef`, per-encoder global `ef`, and a generic geometry-only adaptive baseline inspired by Ada-ef or SHEAF. This could be a useful fixed-index deployment policy, but algorithmic novelty is weak unless the encoder-conditioned signal adds value after those baselines. Account for probe cost. Avoid training on the known-test queries and reporting an in-sample routing gain.

### 3. Lower priority: broader quantizer search or another student

Adding more quantizers, tuning graph build parameters, or retraining Nano may improve an operational frontier but obscures the clean question about frozen document graphs. The existing E2 grid has four quantization modes and enough evidence to choose 1-bit as a controlled case. New model training is unnecessary for the proposed mechanism test.

## Limits

The one-million subset preserves positives but removes many competing documents. Its ANN penalty may differ in magnitude or sign on full MS MARCO. FiQA is a useful second check at 57,638 documents, but a two-dataset finding should retain domain and scale limits. “Off manifold” is a hypothesis; Zero's lower top-1 cosine is not an off-manifold proof. The sources above show that OOD ANN and adaptive search are well explored, while no source in this focused seven-paper check directly tests a static/transformer/teacher query-tier swap over one frozen text document graph. That limited search does not establish global absence.
