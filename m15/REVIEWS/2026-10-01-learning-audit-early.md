# Early learning audit (M0–M8)

Purpose: candidate **decisions** for an applied search-engineering paper, not an inventory of experiments. The numbers below are historical and local to the stated datasets, models, index, and recipes; later milestones may supersede them. “Controlled” means a matched or cross-fitted comparison within this project, not external replication.

## Strongest candidates for the synthesis

| **Select the teacher on the student artifact, then on retrieval.** A high-scoring teacher can yield a poor cheap query encoder. | Eight closed-form tables had Spearman **0.000** between teacher ceiling and table retrieval; the best teacher ranked fifth as a table. An arctic teacher selected by its ceiling was withdrawn when its table scored **−0.0480** versus the incumbent. M8's granite/gte screen again found poorer tables (**−0.052/−0.109**). It ranks teacher-plus-index pairs, not teacher behavior on an already fixed index. |
| **Vector imitation and training loss are unreliable proxies for retrieval.** | Higher ridge λ raised cosine agreement while nDCG fell; the highest-cosine candidate ranked sixth of ten on retrieval. In an objective run, loss fell **0.51→0.13** while dev retrieval fell at every check. [M7 findings](../../m7/FINDINGS.md), [M7 results](../../m7/RESULTS.md). | Gate distillation choices on actual ranking with the intended document vectors, not cosine to teacher queries or a declining loss curve. | **Controlled diagnostic, recipe-specific.** The loss example is one objective/run; it does not indict all contrastive objectives. TREC-COVID showed no visible penalty, with only 50 queries. | **Budget the whole retrieval system, including the alternative that already has cheap queries.** | In six named datasets, OpenSearch doc-sparse scored **0.4868** with **0.018 ms** query processing versus LightRetriever dense websearch **0.4320** at **0.023 ms**; LightRetriever hybrid had an unresolved reproduction deficit. ## Useful supporting lessons, lower priority for the main story

- **Post-hoc linear alignment did not buy compatibility.** Even oracle test-selected regularization gave potion-32M→arctic **0.3280**, below potion's own-index **0.3427**; potion-8M gave **0.3036**. [M1–M6 findings](../../research/m1-m6-findings.md), [M8 explored](../../m8/EXPLORED.md). **Controlled negative** for these static encoders and a linear map, not a proof about nonlinear or jointly trained alignment. Action: budget a real retrieval test before assuming a dimension-matching adapter permits an index swap.
- **A cheap lexical channel beat most table tuning.** M8 reports M7 fusion **+0.057** over its dense score and fused **0.4911** versus OpenSearch **0.4868**, while M8 table levers improved <~0.005. [M8 findings](../../m8/FINDINGS.md). **Descriptive/recipe-specific:** fusion has its own storage and retrieval costs; do not infer a universal “always fuse” policy. Action: compare lexical fusion early, alongside its operational cost, rather than treating it as a cosmetic late ablation.
- **Interface parity can dominate model changes.** Omitting BOS when generating LightRetriever table rows cost ~**4 points** mean, **11.8** on ArguAna; restoring adapter tokenizer behavior recovered SciFact **.642→.663**, ArguAna **.394→.518**, SciDocs **.147→.176**. [M1–M6 findings](../../research/m1-m6-findings.md). **Controlled reconstruction failure.** Action: record tokenizer, special tokens, prompt, pooling, dtype, and self-hit rules with the vectors; replay reference cells before interpreting model deltas. The same archive records 50/58 official overlap cells within .001 only after harness corrections.
- **Do algebra before training extra query-side transforms.** Centering, whitening, principal-component removal, and per-token scalar weights can be folded into a freely learned table; they add no representational capacity. Document-side linear maps cease to be absorbable after document L2 normalization. [M7 findings](../../m7/FINDINGS.md), [M8 findings](../../m8/FINDINGS.md). **Exact under the stated pooling/normalization assumptions.** Action: distinguish a useful training prior from a new serving capability.
- **Use the real frequency distribution to test a large-vocabulary solver.** A dense 65K-row fp64 Gram would need **34.4 GB**; block CG used **4.42 GB / 10.4 s**. Unpreconditioned CG failed to converge after 1,500 iterations on Zipfian counts; Jacobi converged in 61, whereas a uniform synthetic test passed in 131 unpreconditioned iterations. [M8 results](../../m8/RESULTS.md), [M8 explored](../../m8/EXPLORED.md). **Controlled engineering result, niche to table fitting.** Action: include skewed token counts in feasibility tests; do not reject a candidate teacher merely because the naive Gram does not fit memory.

## Editorial liabilities and cuts

1. **Do not collapse three questions.** Teacher selection changes the document index; query replacement holds it fixed; deployment changes ANN/search cost. The early teacher results were *teacher-plus-own-index* comparisons. A paper centered on a fixed existing index cannot use them as evidence that a different teacher can be swapped in without reindexing.
2. **Avoid a universal ceiling claim.** M8's gap to a co-adapted LightRetriever system was **−0.0243** and its measured query-side levers did not close it. Yet listwise training, full document co-adaptation, and trained vocabulary changes were not run. The honest finding is a bounded failure map, not that frozen-index distillation cannot work.
3. **Do not use M7/M8 dev outcomes as fresh validation.** The archive explicitly counts heavy reuse and records train-adjacent gains, step sensitivity, reversed proxy order, and an uninformative small-fit “class ceiling” probe disowned in M8. They are valuable lessons about selection, not independent headline performance proof.
4. **Keep intricate methodological and algebraic details out of the lead.** The solver, compositional row algebra, registration mechanics, and query-length paired-sample correction are useful appendix material. The actionable main story is: choose on the shipped artifact; evaluate ranking rather than surrogate agreement; price ANN/index/fusion effects; test whether a plausible lever actually moves retrieval.
