# Late milestone learning audit — 2026-10-01

Scope: the committed narrative and aggregate summaries for M17–M21, not protected raw evaluation material. This is an editorial inventory for a paper aimed at search engineers. The strength labels assess what these records support, not whether a result deserves a main-text slot. No new experiment or literature search was performed for this audit.

## Best transferable lessons

| Priority | Learning and actual evidence | Production decision | Strength and limit | Paper use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Measure the served retrieval route, not only dense retrieval.** M18's specialized query table reached a best development dense gain of **+0.007591 nDCG@10** (paired 95% interval **[-0.000844, +0.018995]**) but its best Zero+BM25 fused gain was only **+0.002953** (interval **[0, +0.008858]**) against a registered **+0.010** fused gate. M20's descriptive BEIR-15 rows show fusion's direction depends on the corpus: HotpotQA Zero dense **.6127 → .7015** with BM25, while MS MARCO Nano dense **.4063 → .3699** with BM25. Sources: [M18 findings](../../m18/FINDINGS.md), [M18 status](../../m18/STATUS.md), [M20 status](../../m20/STATUS.md). | Select and tune a query encoder on the actual dense/lexical serving route and its target slice; do not promote a table from a dense-only gain. | **Strong as an observed decision failure, limited as a general rule.** M18 is a 100-query internal development surface with a model judge; its dense interval crosses zero. M20 is descriptive, with known teacher exposure on some sets. Fusion settings, lexical strength, and corpus matter. | Main-text production lesson, short example and explicit endpoint. This adds operational relevance without adding another long benchmark table. |
| 2 | **A query-side vocabulary patch can have an exact blast radius even when its relevance gain is unresolved.** M19 built 12 added term rows deterministically. Bare-term int8 cosine to the intended construction was at least **.9999541**; no-roster-term queries inherited v1 bit-identically. Added resident cost was **12 × 1,028 bytes**; on 10,000 queries, encoder median/p95 latency ratios were **.9580/1.0370** and hybrid ratios **.9956/.9696**. But relevance promotion stopped: 60 dev queries had 93 unknown labels; exact label enumeration put T0–v1 short-context P@10 delta at **[+.016667, +.075000]** around the **+.03** gate. Sources: [M19 findings](../../m19/FINDINGS.md), [M19 status](../../m19/STATUS.md), [M17–M19 postmortem](../../research/m17-m19-postmortem-2026-09-13.md). | Use deterministic, term-triggered patching when the operational requirement is bounded change without reindexing; establish adequate judgments before claiming better retrieval. | **Strong for inheritance, cost, and runtime; inconclusive for quality.** The audit agreement of **832/852** known items cannot resolve the 93 unknowns, and the same model family supplied original and audit judgments. Do not present this as a successful relevance intervention or an independent human audit. | A compact “what can be patched safely” case study or engineering sidebar; explicitly mark relevance inconclusive. |
| 3 | **Small apparent gains require an evaluation instrument built for small gains.** In M18, 344 prospective query–artifact pairs yielded only 140 clear labels (142 partial, 62 bad). Query-bearing source chunks had to be excluded: before filtering, source objects appeared in top-10 for **97.5% of BM25** and **100% of dense** queries. M19's auditor found 60 items unjudgeable without passage context; one binary flip could move a term by **.0333**, exceeding its **+.03** gate. Sources: [M18 findings](../../m18/FINDINGS.md), [M19 findings](../../m19/FINDINGS.md), [postmortem](../../research/m17-m19-postmortem-2026-09-13.md). | Before training or promotion, inspect query provenance, remove source-object leakage, allow “unsure,” provide passage context, and compute the effect of one label on the gate. | **Strong as a documented evaluation failure mode, not as a new estimator.** The fractions apply to this internal project-memory surface. A high nominal agreement rate does not repair a gate crossed by plausible unknown-label assignments. | Main-text evaluation-design paragraph; much more transferable than recounting every M17–M19 experiment. |
| 4 | **Training progress is not retrieval progress.** M17's five 4,000-step joint/listwise screen arms ended at **.5807–.5938 nDCG@10**, each below the same-surface untrained V0 **.6153**, even as training and held-out losses fell. M18 repeated the milder form: a dense gain failed the fused production gate. Sources: [M17 status](../../m17/STATUS.md), [M17 findings](../../m17/FINDINGS.md), [postmortem](../../research/m17-m19-postmortem-2026-09-13.md). | Keep a same-surface retrieval checkpoint and the served-route gate in a cheap screen before extending training. | **Strong negative result for these objectives and data; mechanism unisolated.** It does not establish that listwise training or vocabulary extension generally fails. M17's untrained V0 is the relevant comparator, not an arbitrary older release. | One concise negative-result paragraph, ideally alongside the paper's teacher-screen evidence. |
| 5 | **Estimate encoder throughput by tokens and batch shape.** M20's corpus throughput ranged **41–887 documents/s** (21.6×), while token throughput ranged **12,920–15,774 tokens/s** (1.22×). A 59-doc/s early projection implied ~163 hours; the Nano tower finished in **39.9 hours**, inside the token-based **36.9–45.4-hour** range. A separate 50,000-document length-sorted encode call caused CUDA caching-allocator reservation to reach **23.06 GiB** on a **10.24-GiB** card for **1.76 GiB** live tensors, with a severe throughput collapse and no useful retry signal; `expandable_segments:True` bounded reservation to **4.40 GiB** and the reproduced shard was byte-for-byte identical at **179 docs/s**. Sources: [M20 findings](../../m20/FINDINGS.md), [M20 status](../../m20/STATUS.md). | Cost an index build from representative token distributions; test changing batch shapes and allocator behavior, not only the maximum sequence length. | **Strong case study on one stack; not a universal speed ratio or allocator prescription.** The 1.22× token range and fix should remain tied to this workload/hardware. | High-value build-cost sidebar or separate engineering article. It makes “cheap query encoder” concrete by discussing the hidden document-index build. |
| 6 | **The embedding interface includes dtype and normalization order.** M21 traced FastEmbed `mean_pooling`'s int64 mask × float32 embeddings to float64 NumPy output across 16 routed models, doubling 1024-d query-vector bytes. Two plausible narrowing fixes preserved float32 parity but broke float16: a fully attended 1024-d vector of 10 overflowed the float16 squared norm to infinity and normalized to zeros. Moving the cast after normalization preserved the upstream accumulation and reached minimum true cosine **1.0** against Torch/ORT fixtures, with maximum absolute difference **1.1548e-7**. Sources: [M21 status](../../m21/STATUS.md), [M21 benchmarks](../../m21/BENCHMARKS.md), [project status](../../PROJECT_STATUS.md). | Contract-test dtype, padding, mask arithmetic, normalization, and low-precision edge cases across training and serving runtimes. | **Strong concrete interoperability bug and repair.** M21 adds no new relevance result. Project status says registrations merged upstream; the dtype fix had not yet merged as of its September 30 snapshot, so do not imply otherwise. | Practical implementation sidebar; keep out of the core retrieval claim. |

## Additional findings worth a sentence, if space permits

- **Vocabulary support is a data constraint.** M17 found 210,447 candidate fragments/terms in a 600,000-query pool and selected 445; 199,763 failed its minimum support of 20 distinct source documents and 50 contexts. Splitting `s3` or `k8s` into subwords is not the same as being out of vocabulary, and a new row does not by itself supply meaning. The practical choice is to inspect fragmentation *and licensed contextual support* before adding terms. [M17 status](../../m17/STATUS.md), [M17 findings](../../m17/FINDINGS.md). **Correction:** an old sentence in `m17/FINDINGS.md` attributes Nano's 35M cap to Zero; `m17/STATUS.md` corrects that Zero is bounded by licensed support and resident size/latency. Do not repeat the old cap claim.
- **Frozen-index specialization has more than one failure mode.** M17's jointly trained general table lost quality; M18's specialized table improved dense development but missed the served fusion gate; M19's deterministic patch preserved unaffected queries but did not clear an evidence gate. These are three distinct outcomes, not one proof that query-table updates never work. [Postmortem](../../research/m17-m19-postmortem-2026-09-13.md).
- **Aggregate rankings hide domain reversals.** In the descriptive BEIR-15 evaluation, bge-small surpassed Stella on HotpotQA and climate-fever, and Nano's FEVER result was far behind comparators. Disclosed teacher exposure means FEVER cannot establish the cause of the reversal. Query-resampling intervals do not cover seed or training-run variation. Use per-domain rows to decide deployment, without claiming contamination caused the gap. [M20 status](../../m20/STATUS.md), [M20 findings](../../m20/FINDINGS.md).
- **A “validated” benchmark requires a precise evidence label.** M20's reserved four were spent once and are now known-test; its BEIR-15 extension is descriptive, not a second confirmation. M21's benchmark contract keeps exact quality separate from ANN/latency, dense separate from fusion, and clean-four separate from teacher-exposed all-six. Nano–LEAF was positive on all-six but **unresolved** on clean-four; Zero–BM25's positive raw interval did not pass the Holm family threshold. Do not call these ties or superiority. [M20 status](../../m20/STATUS.md), [M21 benchmarks](../../m21/BENCHMARKS.md), [project status](../../PROJECT_STATUS.md).
- **An internal search case can validate a system without validating an encoder improvement.** M18 indexed 79,269 natural-language units from a pinned project snapshot and delivered a Zero+BM25 DBSF@100 endpoint. Its 40-query confirmation reported fusion Recall@10 **.300** versus BM25 **.200** and Zero dense **.220**, while fusion nDCG@10 **.151445** was slightly below Zero dense **.153176** and the 10-query error/troubleshooting slice scored zero on every route. The small, model-judged snapshot cannot justify a general production-quality claim. [M18 findings](../../m18/FINDINGS.md), [M18 status](../../m18/STATUS.md).

## What should stay out of the M15 headline

1. **A claim that M17–M19 produced an improved encoder.** None did: M17 closed with no survivor, M18 with a working system but no promoted table, M19 at an unresolved judgment boundary. [Postmortem](../../research/m17-m19-postmortem-2026-09-13.md).
2. **A claim that targeted jargon patching improves search.** M19 proves scoped behavior and low operational cost, but the quality interval straddles its registered gate. Bare-term cosine and a few attractive examples are not relevance evidence. [M19 findings](../../m19/FINDINGS.md).
3. **A claim that fusion always helps or that a benchmark average predicts a site's result.** M20 includes positive and negative dataset-level fusion deltas. The paper should show the served route used for each statement. [M20 status](../../m20/STATUS.md).
4. **A direct comparison of asset size, resident RAM, and latency across different protocols.** M18's int8-on-disk v1 loader expanded to ~125 MB float32 in RAM; M21 distinguishes Zero's original one-core **.38 ms** from common four-thread **.1119 ms**, and 94 MB release artifact from 31.8 MB query ONNX and 90.1 MiB measured NumPy/tokenizer residency. State the object and measurement context beside every number. [M18 findings](../../m18/FINDINGS.md), [M21 benchmarks](../../m21/BENCHMARKS.md).
5. **A fully closed M20 archive.** Measurements are complete, but the object-storage copy was outstanding in the project status. [Project status](../../PROJECT_STATUS.md).

## Editorial recommendation

The most useful organizing question is: **What can an engineer improve when the document vectors and index are already deployed?** The established answer is to evaluate compatible query encoders and lexical fusion on the actual serving route and its important slices. M19 supplies a narrow, reproducible query-side patch with a measured blast radius, while honestly demonstrating how hard it is to prove a tiny quality gain. M20 and M21 supply short build/serving pitfalls. This is a stronger production story than a chronological recap of M17–M21, and it preserves the current paper's core frozen-document-index result.

The M17–M19 negative sequence is valuable if summarized as a design boundary: optimization loss, teacher fidelity, local dense improvement, and serving quality are different tests. Its exact training arms, governance history, and speculative causal diagnoses belong in a linked retrospective, not the main M15 narrative.
