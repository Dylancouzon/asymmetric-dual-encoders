# Literature check for M15 (2026-10-01)

Scope: `m15/PAPER.md` v10, `m15/RELATED_WORK.md`, `m15/NOVELTY.md`, `instructions-m15.md`, and `CLAUDE.md`. This is a primary-source check of the paper's framing and printed references, not a systematic review. I did not edit the paper or inspect protected evaluation material.

## Essential finding: distinguish the teacher-choice claim from NanoVDR

[NanoVDR, Appendix H](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12824v3) explicitly calls teacher quality the strongest positive predictor of student retention: Pearson `r=+0.607` across **19 English-only ViDoRe datasets**, holding **one Qwen3-VL teacher and one student** fixed. It relates *dataset-level teacher nDCG@5* to *dataset-level retention*. M15's Spearman `+0.09` relates *different frozen text-embedding teachers* to the ridge table each produces, evaluated over the same six BEIR datasets. The independent variable and unit of analysis differ. This is a useful scientific contrast, not a contradiction or a refutation of NanoVDR. `m15/RELATED_WORK.md` §5 currently says NanoVDR has one teacher and “does not test whether teacher retrieval quality predicts student quality”; revise to specify **across teachers**. The paper's prior-work paragraph should mention NanoVDR's positive within-teacher, across-dataset result when introducing M15's across-teacher result.

## Closest overlap and viable contribution

- [pyNIFE README](https://github.com/stephantul/pynife) already constructs static token vectors aligned to an unchanged teacher, reuses its index, and explicitly proposes dynamically choosing the static or large query model. It even speculates that static models hit a contextualization ceiling. Therefore construction, index reuse, tiering, edge viability, and the intuition that some queries need context are prior art. M15 can contribute the controlled 26-tower screen, closed-form fitting route, registered quality/cost evidence, per-query quantification, router limits, and measured ANN interaction.
- [LightRetriever](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12260v5) already reports a lookup-and-average dense query path, 15 BEIR datasets, roughly 95% retention, >1000-fold query-encoding speedup and >10-fold end-to-end throughput. Its method trains a query-side LLM-derived token representation with contrastive relevance supervision, and the document retriever is trained as part of that system. This differs from a closed-form fit into an already pretrained, fixed third-party document tower. Do not describe full BEIR breadth, fast lookup, or end-to-end measurement alone as new. Its training uses 8.38M samples and 8 H800/A800 GPUs; the M15 few-minute teacher screen is a plausible practical distinction, but quantify actual fit and screening costs separately if that is a lead claim.
- [LEAF](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12539v2) offers a 23M student aligned to a teacher, teacher-document/student-query asymmetric deployment, precomputed teacher embeddings, an L2-only loss, black-box compatibility, no relevance judgments or hard negatives, and says infrastructure needs are modest. [QED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11550) already calls its method a simple recipe and obtains 92.5% BEIR retention with a two-layer student. [NanoVDR](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12824v3) also trains from cached teacher query embeddings with no document processing and reports under 13 GPU-hours. Hence “cheap/easy to train a smaller query model” is too broad for the M15 lead. A much more specific useful question is **how to choose the document tower for future cheap-query service before paying the indexing cost**. M15's evidence only supports that for its static ridge recipe and measured teachers/datasets; it does not establish the same teacher ranking for the nano tier.
- [EmbedDistill](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12005) and [QED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11550) predate frozen-document asymmetric distillation. [Cho and Hariharan](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/html/Cho_On_the_Efficacy_of_Knowledge_Distillation_ICCV_2019_paper.html) and [PROD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13335) predate the general observation that a stronger teacher can make a worse student. The new empirical result is the controlled *embedding-tower ranking failure for a fixed, static query recipe*, with a cheap predictive screen.
- [Arabzadeh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10739) establish cost-aware per-query routing, though between sparse/dense/hybrid systems. M15's matched-budget oracle and shared-index cheap/expensive query-encoder router is a narrower but credible distinction. [Faggioli et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09947) supports caution about neural QPP; it is stronger and more relevant than a self-cited Qdrant blog for the generic QPP sentence.

## Cautions for paper language

- `PAPER.md` §2 currently says “The architecture comes from prior work [pyNIFE, LightRetriever].” True but incomplete: LEAF, QED, and EmbedDistill establish the frozen-document asymmetric student, while pyNIFE establishes teacher-index static reuse. Cite the architecture by component rather than two names alone.
- LightRetriever's roughly 95% retention and M15's 81.4% are not controlled comparisons: teachers, training supervision/data, and query/document training differ. Do not attribute the gap solely to co-training; that is a plausible inference, not an isolated result.
- `m15/NOVELTY.md` says “Every published asymmetric pair trains its cheap query tower against the document encoder it will retire alongside.” This is too sweeping given QED, EmbedDistill, LEAF, NanoVDR and pyNIFE. Replace with the defensible observation that the cited papers do not perform M15's controlled *cross-teacher screen for a fixed static fit*.
- A claim of no earlier query-vector blending paper is a limited-search observation; absence is not verified by the sources checked here. The negative blend result can stand without a priority claim.
