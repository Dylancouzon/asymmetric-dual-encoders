# Owner Reader Review of M15 Draft v10

2026-10-01. Read only except this review. Standard: the M15 goals in `instructions-m15.md`: new IR knowledge, a paper worth reading, checkable claims, honest Qdrant engineering, and a lean result. The owner wants a substantive rewrite and is not attached to the current contents.

## Verdict

The experiments are unusually careful, but the paper still has no unmistakable goal. It starts by asking how to choose a document tower and which queries to route, then spends substantial space on table expressivity, model comparisons, vector blending, prefix behavior, and ANN settings. These are individually valid, but a reader cannot tell which decision the paper exists to improve. I would not publish v10 with **Screen the Student** as the headline. The screen predicts the quality of one *closed-form probe*, while the served Zero uses a different trained recipe tested on Stella alone. Also, an engineer with an already frozen index cannot choose its teacher. Those limits make the present title's practical instruction larger than the evidence.

The stronger paper asks: **Can we make queries cheaper without rebuilding the document index, and how do we determine the real quality and cost before deploying?** It has a concrete sequence: assess whether the index's vector space admits a useful cheap student; account for preparation and fitting; measure the student under exact and approximate search; then choose a tier at a stated quality requirement. Tower choice belongs to the planning case, while a probe of the fixed tower belongs to the existing-index case. Name the distinction in the introduction. This is a useful research and engineering paper, not a claim that the architecture is new.

## What a Reader Could Cite or Use

1. **Teacher retrieval quality fails as a static-student selection rule in this measured roster.** The highest-scoring registered tower, gte-large-en-v1.5, scores 0.5970 itself but produces a 0.2455 table; Stella scores 0.5745 itself and produces a 0.3974 table, a 0.1519 table-score difference. Across 26 checkpoints, tower-versus-table rank correlation is +0.09, whereas development-forum table-versus-public-table correlation is +0.88. This is the paper's clearest counterexample and a defensible *probe selection* result. State that 16 towers were added exploratorily, the registered ten are the confirmatory core, the checkpoints share a tokenizer, and a screen can incur nonzero clean-4 selection regret (0.0278 over 26 in the later audit). Do not turn this into a universal claim about teacher size or all distilled students. The Spearman interval for tower quality includes meaningful positive correlations, so “did not rank” describes these samples rather than proof of zero predictive value.
2. **Cheap query vectors can cost more to retrieve.** At unquantized HNSW `ef=16` on the positive-preserving one-million-passage subset, Zero loses 16.3% of its exact nDCG@10, Nano 7.0%, and Stella 4.4%. To stay within 1% of *each tier's own* exact quality, Qdrant needs `ef=512` for Zero versus `ef=256` for Nano in the selected quantized configurations. The roughly 60-fold encode advantage over Nano shrinks to 2.2-fold end to end at that target (3.1-fold at the 5% target). This is a useful systems observation that distinguishes the work from another distillation comparison. The 1% targets are **tier-relative**, so the two latency points do not deliver equal absolute quality; Figure 3's absolute quality/latency view is essential. The farther-from-documents cosine is a plausible clue, not an established mechanism.
3. **The table's loss is concentrated, but current routers do not exploit it well.** The shuffled-query diagnostic localizes roughly 90% of the Stella-minus-Zero gap to the 40% of queries on which shuffling hurts Stella. Because that group is defined using relevance judgments and shuffling changes more than syntax, it is an exploratory explanation, not a serving rule or proof of a causal share. The best inexpensive pre-search routing signals barely help; the post-search margin router recovers about a quarter of oracle headroom while scoring 0.4865 versus 0.5317 for always-Nano on its six-set evaluation. That is worth a candid paragraph, not a second headline or a routing section equal in weight to the teacher and engine findings.

The likeliest research rejection is “valid but diffuse engineering”: query-side distillation and frozen-index inference are prior art; selecting a model on a judged development set is unsurprising; and the central screen has not been shown to select the trained table that the paper deploys. The later 16 checkpoints and one index/one engine deployment limit generalization. The answer is a precise decision protocol and two memorable empirical counterexamples, not a longer novelty inventory or more weak proxy tests. A contrasting-tower run of the **served** table recipe would be the only new experiment I would prioritize if the paper wants the stronger selection claim. Without it, call the closed-form screen a fast triage probe and keep the extrapolation explicit.

## Proposed Rewrite

**Title:** *Can You Make Queries Cheap Without Rebuilding the Index?*

**Abstract structure:** (1) The decision: document vectors are expensive to replace, but query cost recurs. (2) Method: a fixed-index three-tier study plus a closed-form probe across ten registered and 16 exploratory towers. (3) Counterexample: the strongest tower in the registered roster yields its weakest table, while development performance of the table ranks other tables. (4) Deployment consequence: Zero and Nano retain 81.4% and 90.5% of Stella's BEIR-15 exact-search nDCG@10 at 0.044 and 2.25 ms encode p50; Qdrant search reduces Zero's roughly 60-fold encode advantage over Nano to 2.2-3.1-fold at tier-relative quality targets on the one-million-passage diagnostic. (5) Boundary: the probe has not been validated for the separately trained served table, and the systems result is one index and engine. Exclude router, shuffle, size correlation, blend, and competitor claims from the abstract. This gives the reader one question, one selection surprise, and one deployment surprise.

**Body:** Introduction poses the decision and distinguishes an existing fixed index from choosing a future document tower. Setup defines the alignment contract, data roles, and the *full workflow cost* (teacher target/data preparation, document-vector availability, fitting, query encoding, and search). Result 1 shows the tower/table reversal and the screen's rank and regret, with Figure 1. Result 2 shows the actual Stella tiers and build costs, including that Nano's 57.3 A100 hours/about $95 covers the training loop at the recorded rate rather than upstream preparation, and Zero's 20-minute fit follows roughly 8-12 hours of teacher encoding. Result 3 shows exact-to-ANN degradation and absolute quality against end-to-end latency in Qdrant, with Figure 3 and per-tier settings. A short “failure boundary” paragraph can explain shuffle sensitivity and why current routing does not erase the loss. Keep the registered contrasts in full in Appendix B, as required.

**Cut aggressively:** Move the Section 4 family and proxy tables, the Section 5 absorbability proof and most perturbation detail, the Section 6 router inventory and Figure 2, Section 6.3's blend result, the prefix table, and the main-text benchmark/comparator narration to appendices or a compact limitations paragraph. The one algebraic observation about post-pooling transforms needs at most a sentence in the body. Keep one worked query only if it shows the judged document and what each system retrieved; the current ranks alone do not let a reader see the failure. Remove “search-as-you-type” from the introduction unless the paper uses realistic typing traces; cut BEIR prefixes do not establish that use case.

## Three Changes With the Highest Return

1. **Lead with the decision and redraw the paper around it.** Present one compact workflow figure or table: what input an engineer needs, what each stage costs, what number decides whether to continue, and where a fixed index constrains choice. Existing receipts support this; the new cost audit should prevent the attractive training-loop dollars and minutes from being mistaken for total reproduction cost.
2. **Turn the tower screen into a bounded rule with a visible consequence.** Use the gte-large/Stella reversal, the clean-4 regret, and the registered/exploratory split. Phrase the advice as “probe the candidate index's table on judged development queries before committing to table training or relying on tower quality.” If the index is already built, this is a feasibility check, not a teacher-selection method. A served-recipe run on a contrasting tower would justify the stronger teacher-selection wording.
3. **Make the Qdrant measurement a reusable search-engine lesson.** Put absolute nDCG@10 against end-to-end latency and exact-versus-ANN loss ahead of the configuration sweep. State the one-million positive-preserving subset, hardware/runtime, and tier-relative target beside the result. Explain that the engine lets one collection serve the tiers and tune their ANN work, without implying this capability is unique to Qdrant. Honest publication of the poor Zero ANN behavior and the reproducible settings will do more for Qdrant's IR reputation than a product claim.
