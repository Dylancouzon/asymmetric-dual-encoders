## Reader review

The construction and serving spine holds, but the paper starts to read like stitched findings when Section 5 adds fusion and routing after the two claims. I would cite the teacher-selection reversal **for the tested recipes**. I would argue about the search-cost finding, but share it more readily with the recovery result, relevance result, and censoring visible together.

1. **Section 4, opening and “Across 25 teacher spaces”: serving mechanism is overstated.** “The graph index was built for the teacher’s [queries]” gives HNSW a query-dependent construction the described experiment does not establish; the indexes are built over document vectors. The robust result is lower recovery of *each path’s own* exact neighbors at equal `ef`. Recast the mechanism as a hypothesis, lead with that measured recovery deficit, and show the number of unreachable targets beside the median 4× `ef`. State immediately that the prespecified relevance-loss measure agrees on FiQA and is mixed elsewhere. That would make the finding citable without implying 4× latency or a general relevance penalty.

2. **Section 3, “A second student recipe” and conclusion: replication language exceeds the design.** The “independent student” shares the fit list, teacher indexes, selection order, and a closed-form ridge fit with the table screen. “There is no single best teacher for cheap students” reaches beyond two related recipes; the paper also reports positive within-width associations. Call this a *second student representation under the same screening setup*. Replace the universal conclusion with: teacher score alone ranked these students poorly; the preferred teacher depended on the tested recipe. Keep the concrete 0.325 versus 0.223 head choice next to that claim.

3. **Sections 3–4: make the limits of the two findings easier to retain.** Finding A’s paragraph moves through all-26 correlations, registered-ten correlations, a width qualifier, and fidelity observations before its recommendation. Finding B’s main result paragraph packs recovery, relevance, censoring, corpus-size speculation, and a defense of measure order into one passage. Give each finding its result and one caveat sentence in the body; put the remaining qualifications in Section 6 or the appendices. Expand HNSW at first use and explain `ef` as search effort before presenting its multiplier.

4. **Section 5 and length: cut about 450 words from the 3,461-word main text.** Fusion and routing are useful consequences of a shared index, but their detailed scores, operator depth, failed blend, and oracle policy create a third results section. Reduce Section 5 to one short paragraph pointing to Appendix A; move the query-precision numbers and exploratory geometry detail from Section 4 to Appendix C. Shorten Section 1’s prior-work inventory. This preserves the two findings and gets close to 3,000 words.

5. **Figures 1–5 and Table 1: give the central claims the visual space.** Table 1 earns its place as setup. Figures 2 and 3 support Finding A but could be paired panels with the correlation table in the caption. Figure 5 shows the equal-`ef` deficit yet omits the multiplier and censoring behind the headline; add them there. Figure 4 mixes compression configurations, making the serving-cost comparison hard to read; separate the uncompressed comparison or move the mixed-configuration curves to the appendix. Figure 1 is useful context but is the first candidate to move if space remains tight.

**Strongest passage:** Section 3’s “strongest registered teacher … produces the weakest registered table” gives a concrete, disputable reversal that makes the screening result worth reading.
