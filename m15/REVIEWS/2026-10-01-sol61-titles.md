   Risk: Accurate but less memorable than the current title.

2. **Encoder Width and Retrieval Quality Predict Closed-Form Query Students over Frozen Indexes**  
   Emphasises: The conditional relationship and held-out prediction.  
   Risk: Leaves the HNSW finding outside the title.

3. **Retrieval Quality and HNSW Search Effort under Query Encoder Substitution**  
   Emphasises: The two consequences of replacing the query path.  
   Risk: “Substitution” obscures distillation and the frozen-index constraint.

4. **An Empirical Study of Query-Side Distillation across 26 Frozen Encoder Spaces**  
   Emphasises: The breadth of the comparison under one fitting protocol.  
   Risk: Names the study design without revealing its findings.

**(b) Memorable claim with a sober subtitle**

5. **The Index Stays; the Search Budget Grows: Query-Side Distillation over Frozen Document Vectors**  
   Emphasises: Preserving the index still changes the work required at search time.  
   Risk: Foregrounds neighbor recovery; relevance-based penalties are less consistent.

6. **Teacher Quality Needs a Width Adjustment: Predicting Closed-Form Query Students over Frozen Indexes**  
   Emphasises: Teacher quality becomes predictive only conditional on width.  
   Risk: “Adjustment” may suggest a corrective training method rather than statistical conditioning.

7. **The Encoding Speedup Is Only Part of the Saving: Query Distillation and HNSW Effort over Frozen Indexes**  
   Emphasises: Encoding and graph search must enter the same cost accounting.  
   Risk: The hook is longer and less sharp than the current one.

8. **The Strongest Teacher Can Leave a Weak Student: Width and Search Effort in Frozen-Index Query Distillation**  
   Emphasises: The teacher-selection surprise alongside the deployment cost.  
   Risk: Readers may expect a causal explanation of the teacher–student gap.

**(c) Questions or mild provocations**

9. **What Does a Cheaper Query Encoder Cost over a Frozen Document Index?**  
   Emphasises: The central engineering question, encompassing quality and search effort.  
   Risk: Could sound like a latency study rather than an empirical IR study.

10. **When Does a Better Retrieval Space Yield a Better Query Student?**  
    Emphasises: The failed pooled heuristic and its conditional recovery.  
    Risk: Omits both the frozen-index constraint and the graph-search finding.

11. **Can the Query Encoder Change without Changing the Search Budget?**  
    Emphasises: The hidden consequence of retaining the same HNSW graph.  
    Risk: Does not identify distillation or distinguish effort from latency.

12. **Is the Strongest Retrieval Space the Best Home for a Cheap Query Encoder?**  
    Emphasises: Compatibility with an existing space and the teacher-selection reversal.  
    Risk: “Best home” is mildly informal and may imply the study identifies an optimal space.

My recommendation is **“The Index Stays; the Search Budget Grows: Query-Side Distillation over Frozen Document Vectors.”** It preserves the current title’s hook while naming a measured consequence, and the subtitle gives IR readers a precise setting.
