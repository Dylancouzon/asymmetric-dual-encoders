# Focused Reader Review of M15 Paper v13

2026-10-01. Scope: direction and interest in the abstract, introduction, and new Section 4.2, judged against the goal of an honest applied research paper for search engineers. This is not a numerical or implementation review. No paper edits.

## Verdict

**Yes, the paper is now coherent and interesting for its intended audience.** Its question stays visible from the first sentence through the conclusion: what selectable query compute buys when the document index remains fixed. The memorable result remains the roughly 62-fold Zero/Nano encoding gap becoming 2.2-fold after retrieval on the same binary collection at tier-relative quality targets. That is a system decision, not a scoreboard. The teacher screen and construction cost follow as bounded design evidence, and prior art and unfavorable comparator outcomes remain visible. It does not read like a brochure.

Section 4.2 earns its place. It asks a narrower follow-up to the ANN result: with document sign codes and original-neighbor targets fixed, how much candidate information is lost by signing the *query* as well? The controlled 40-candidate result is substantial for Zero on both workloads, and the fixed-code comparison adds research content beyond another engine sweep. The weaker E17 native replication is reported before E18, so the paper does not disguise it as a successful confirmation. The section correctly says that exhaustive candidate coverage is not native ANN quality, nDCG, or a latency improvement. It offers a reason to inspect query precision, not a production fix or an explanation of the entire graph-search gap.

The abstract is dense because it now names the frontier, ANN effect, precision control, teacher screen, and build accounting. The precision numbers take considerable space, but they occur *after* the shared-index result and their non-native boundary is stated. They do not displace the central argument enough to require another restructure. If tightening for an abstract length limit, compress the two workload-specific coverage changes to one clause about Zero's 6.7–10.8 percentage-point gain at 40 candidates. That is optional compression, not an essential finding.

**No essential reader-direction issue found.** This is a good scoped applied study, not an architectural breakthrough or a demonstrated native precision remedy. The paper says so plainly; more project history or experiments are not needed for this draft.
