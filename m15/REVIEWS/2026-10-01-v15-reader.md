# V15 reader review — production value and narrative

**Judgment.** V15 finally has a paper-sized question a search engineer can use: what happens when query compute changes but document vectors stay fixed? The shared-index quality/cost table and the finding that ANN work narrows Zero's encoding advantage are the strongest reason to read and share it. The 26-checkpoint teacher reversal is separately interesting and citable. It is an applied study with limited workloads, not a claim of architectural novelty or a measured production rollout. Its current weakness is the order and selection of evidence, rather than a missing experiment or glossary.

## Essential changes

1. **Keep the fixed-index decision uninterrupted.** Section 2 establishes the three query paths, then Section 3 spends roughly a full section choosing a teacher *before* any index is built. It carefully explains that this changes the index, but the placement still breaks the paper's promised question. Move the teacher-selection section after the same-index ANN, precision, and fusion results, under an explicit “If building a new index” transition. The fixed-index chain should run: relevance/encoding budget → search budget on the same index → lexical or routing option → actual serving check. The teacher screen remains a substantial second finding, clearly for a different decision.

2. **Turn the shared-binary table into one concrete choice, not only a speed-ratio illustration.** The 62×→2.2× comparison is memorable, but the rows have nDCG **.6116/.6893/.7226**. A reader cannot choose Zero based on a tier-relative 1% recovery target if the application requires, for example, at least **.68** on this diagnostic; then Zero is excluded and Nano is the measured option under a 3 ms sequential budget. A two-sentence worked threshold immediately after the table would show how to apply the result and prevent the abstract's ratio from becoming the takeaway independent of relevance. Label the threshold as illustrative and tied to this sampled diagnostic, not a deployment SLA. This needs no new numbers or run.

3. **Cut the project-memory label/12-term patch passage from the main flow.** Section 6 has a valuable actual-caller/device compatibility finding. Its last paragraph abruptly introduces a different internal retrieval application, source-object leakage, a deterministic patch, unknown labels, and an acceptance gate without enough setup for a reader to judge that case. The general advice about adequate judgments is sound, but the case belongs in the companion history or an appendix. Removing it would make the serving contract the clear ending of the main fixed-index argument, and would avoid implying that an inconclusive patch is another Constella performance result. One brief sentence in limitations can preserve the label-sensitivity point.

## What to retain

Keep the unfavorable own-index bge-small/LEAF disclosure, the distinct exact-search versus ANN endpoints, and the native-precision caveat. These prevent the most tempting misreadings of the fast-query result. The fusion-depth reversal and weak routing result belong because they change what an engineer would test when choosing among the compatible paths; they need no additional project chronology. Do not pull more catalog entries into the main text to make it feel comprehensive.
