## Verdict and coherence

Credible, readable empirical whitepaper with a defensible contribution, substantially more than a model-release benchmark. Not yet the best version: individual studies are better explained than their connection. A bounded revision can fix this without another experiment campaign.

Thesis: replacing a query encoder over frozen document vectors creates two constraints, exact retrieval quality and efficient approximate recovery. Neither follows from original-encoder relevance or replacement encoding speed alone.

RQ1 and RQ2 belong together: substantially the same fitted students and spaces are evaluated first under exact retrieval, then on unchanged graphs. This is an experimental connection. Constella is a related deployment illustration, not validation of the dimensionality predictor or proof that the cheap screen selected a successful trained family. The manuscript acknowledges this locally but does not organize its narrative around the distinction. It lacks an ending bringing the studies together.

The cross-space conditional quality result plus repeated ANN measurements are stronger contributions than Constella retention percentages. Prospective head support and LightRetriever replication strengthen them. Query-side distillation, frozen compatibility and graph sensitivity already have precedents; the contribution is bounded empirical regularity, not architectural novelty or contradiction of prior work.

## Essential recommendations

1. Explain the two-stage experiment after the RQs and at transitions: exact retrieval measures representation loss; graph search measures additional approximation loss. Introduce Constella as a separately trained compatible family illustrating combined encoding/search costs. Close with a short synthesis of the conditional quality association, repeated recovery deficit, workload-dependent relevance consequences and compression of Zero's encoding advantage. No new evidence required.
2. Identify the million-passage timing workload before Table 2 and describe its sampling in Appendix C. E2 uses a positive-preserving MS MARCO subset with all judged positives and sampled competitors, and 6,980 development queries. Removing most competitors makes its relevance optimistic relative to the full corpus; ANN effects need not transfer in magnitude or sign. Encoding comes from a separate medium-query sample. This is a missing standalone methods explanation, not missing research.
3. Align RQ2's predictive promise with E22's outcome: student-minus-original recovery gap at fixed ef=64. The study does not predict required ef, equal-relevance cost or latency. The cross-workload error result makes this important.
4. Separate cheap fitting from cheap inference. The frozen-bge head is not demonstrated to cost less than every reference path, including its own backbone and smaller roster models. Call the cross-space fits constrained student representations or linearly fitted query students; reserve measured recurring savings for Constella.
5. State that the Appendix C precision table uses 40 candidates. Replace the idea that an index inherits original retrieval quality from its document encoder: measured relevance belongs to the original query/document pair on the evaluation tasks, whereas dimensionality belongs to the vectors.
