## Verdict and coherence

Worth sharing after bounded structural revision. The central evidence is credible within scope and substantially more valuable than a release benchmark, but the intellectual relationship and final implication remain for the reader to reconstruct.

Thesis: query-only replacement preserves the document representation, which constrains cheap paths twice, through attainable exact retrieval quality and approximate-search effort.

The findings are not random. RQ1 and RQ2 answer parts of independent query-path choice. There is no demonstrated common mechanism linking dimensionality to ANN penalties. Constella uses differently optimized students and does not validate RQ1's predictive model. The right synthesis is two constraints plus a feasibility example, not a unified explanatory theory. Existing sequence is defensible; interpretation matters more than rearrangement.

The combined cross-space result, recurring ANN behavior, prospective head result and LightRetriever replication are the strongest contribution. Original quality alone does not reliably guide these fits, and fixed document vectors do not ensure graph settings transfer.

## Priority recommendations

1. Add a short discussion/closing synthesis before limitations. Explain the frozen-space quality constraint, useful quality/dimensionality forecast with stronger direct development selection, recurring recovery penalties with workload-dependent relevance effects, and separately trained compatible paths whose encoding savings partly move into search. Explicitly delimit trained-instance transfer. Two paragraphs suffice; this is missing explanation, not missing evidence.
2. Align RQ1's framing with its primary absolute student nDCG outcome. The title/question emphasize retention, but Table B predicts absolute quality. A better absolute student can retain a smaller share of a stronger original encoder. Keep retention for explicit ratios/Constella; do not add another statistical outcome just to preserve the heading.
3. Make the introduction's prospective support recipe-specific. The head's prospective association is positive; the table interval spans zero. The opening's plural language compresses that distinction even though §4.3 accurately reports it. No need to clutter the abstract with numbers.
4. Reconsider the owner-selected title: 'Your Index Is Fine, Your Query Encoder Is Not' suggests a defect/replacement recommendation not established. The index itself constrains students and the large encoder retains higher quality. A conditional quality/search-effort hook would better serve credibility; exact wording negotiable. This is the main editorial disagreement.
5. Separate H2's measurable effort hypothesis from its proposed query-placement explanation. Associations do not identify cause; original-query distance ratio predicts better than the change, and LightRetriever recurrence does not isolate mechanism. Interpret original ratio as an index/workload susceptibility marker; report poor prediction by the change. No new causal experiment is needed for a predictive claim.
6. Add a method-to-instance bridge at §6: cheap fits enable controlled breadth; stronger separately trained paths illustrate feasibility in one space. Do not imply RQ1 predicts Nano or that training alone causes the performance difference; recipes, data and evaluation suites differ.
