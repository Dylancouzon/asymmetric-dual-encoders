[~1.5 min] [Follow the document path into the index, then trace each incoming query path.] Start with a passage. Stella runs 24 layers at width 1,024, averages the token states, applies its square dense projection, and normalizes the result. I store the vector in fp16. Tonight's passages are already at the center of the diagram. HNSW links those document vectors, and binary quantization gives me a compact version for the candidate search. A 1,024-dimensional vector takes 128 bytes at one bit per dimension. Across 2.7 million passages that's 343 MB of codes in RAM. The fp16 originals take 5.49 GB on disk for rescoring. Those figures cover the vector data. The graph and sparse index have their own storage. Now follow a query. Stella adds its instruction prompt and uses its usual encoder. Nano starts from bge-small. I mean-pool layers 12, eight, and four in that order, join the 1,152 features, and project into Stella's 1,024 dimensions. Zero tokenizes, gathers dequantized rows, weights them by the square root of their counts, and normalizes the sum. The compatibility test is whether those outputs rank the stored passages well. I can try each encoder against the very same vectors and graph, even on successive requests. I call that family Constella.
