Already Have an Index? Train Your Own

Full recipe: the paper

Costs are Constella's, for a Stella index: Zero trained on one RTX 3080 once its targets existed (computing Stella's targets took 8-12 h); Nano took 57.3 A100-hours. Training sources must allow commercial use of derived weights. Exploratory.

[~1.25 min] Why it matters: the method is not tied to Stella. If you have a frozen index, you can train its cheap query encoders. - [01] Keep your document encoder and your index. Nothing is re-encoded, ever. - [02] Encode a large set of real queries with your current query model, with its prompt and preprocessing. Those vectors are the targets. For Nano, add passages encoded by your document model. - A hosted model works if it returns the same vectors. - Before training, fit a quick lookup table to check your model teaches well (remember gte-large). - [03] Train: - Zero: one row per token of your tokenizer, start from your model's token encodings, align then rank. About 20 minutes on one GPU once the targets exist. For Stella the targets took 8 to 12 hours. - Nano: a small backbone, three pooled layers, a projection to your vector size, trained on your model's vectors. No relevance labels needed. About $95 of A100 time for ours. - [04] Judge on your own queries: exact nDCG@10 per encoder, then sweep ef and oversampling. Then set your floor and let a router pick. - [QR] The paper has every detail: data, losses, steps, and what failed. [Advance to the thank-you slide.]
