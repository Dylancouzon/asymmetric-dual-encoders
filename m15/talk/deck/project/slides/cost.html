Encode the Corpus Once, Pay for Every Query

BEIR NQ, analytic FLOPs (2·P·T + attention): 2,681,468 passages, mean 109 tokens, no prompt, against the mean of the 3,452 NQ test queries with Stella's query prompt. Measured: the index took 5.3 GPU-hours on one RTX 3080; one Stella query takes 20.5 ms on the M5 Pro CPU. Exploratory.

I could go and make tea while that job ran. I'd rather my users didn't have time to do the same. [Point to the grey line.] That's the index: 2.68 million passages, paid for once. [Trace the red line from the origin.] Every question also goes through Stella before Qdrant can search. A question is much shorter than a passage, so each one is cheap. But they never stop. [Point to the crossing.] After about 9.6 million queries I've spent more compute encoding questions than I spent on the whole corpus. At a hundred queries a second, that's about a day. And that encode sits on the path of every request, roughly 20 milliseconds on this laptop, against a quarter of a millisecond for the search itself. If an agent calls search several times to answer one question, those encodes can all sit on the path to the answer. I wanted to make that part cheaper.
