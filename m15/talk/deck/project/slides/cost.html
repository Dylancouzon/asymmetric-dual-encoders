One Encoder, Two Deadlines

BEIR NQ, 2,681,468 passages. Real NQ test query; its judged passage ranks first in exact search (score 0.80). Vectors: first 3 of 1,024 values. Median times on the M5 Pro. Graph schematic. Exploratory.

[~1.5 min] Why it matters: one model does two jobs with opposite deadlines. Only one of them has a user waiting. - [Top path] Passages go through Stella once and come out as vectors: 1,024 numbers each. Tonight's index is 2.68M NQ passages, 5.3 GPU-hours on my desktop (6.5 hours real time, including a crash). Nobody waits; I made tea. - [Graph] Qdrant links those vectors into an HNSW graph: each passage connected to its near neighbours. - [Bottom path] A real NQ question, "who played Joey's agent Estelle on Friends", goes through the very same 435M-parameter model. Six words through the same 24 layers. 20.5 ms on this laptop. - [Red walk] Its vector walks the graph to June Gable, the right answer out of 2.68 million, in 0.23 ms. - So the search is a quarter of a millisecond. The encoder in front of it is about 90 times longer, on every request. - It never stops: at 100 queries/s, query encoding burns a whole index worth of compute every 27 hours. Agents fire several searches per answer. - The top path is a sunk cost. The bottom path is where a cheaper model pays off, if it can still walk the same graph.
