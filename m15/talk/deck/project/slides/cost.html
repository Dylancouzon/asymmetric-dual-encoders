[~1.25 min] [Point to the score, then the two terms in the cost equation.] The score is a dot product between two unit vectors. I can take my time building the document vector because I get to reuse it. Every uncached query needs a fresh vector and a search. If an agent calls search several times, I pay that query cost several times. For scale, I encoded the 2.7 million Wikipedia passages we'll search later on one RTX 3080. At a steady 141 passages per second, that's an estimated 5.3 GPU-hours of encoding. The run took 6.5 hours elapsed with a memory spill and a crash. Mine was a desktop, and it had opinions. On the query side, Stella saw a median of 31 tokens including its instruction prompt. That's about 25 billion floating-point operations and 32 milliseconds on a laptop CPU with four threads. The mean gives about 10.3 elapsed hours for a million sequential queries, before search. A swap of both encoders brings the passage job back and can leave you running two indexes during migration.
