Zero Trains on a Desktop GPU Against a Frozen Index

one RTX 3080

≈ 20 min to train

Zero: phase 1 16,000 steps × 512, phase 2 2,500 steps InfoNCE, τ 0.02; retrain time is a historical estimate; a new teacher needs 8-12 h of target encoding. All training sources permit commercial derived weights.

[~2 min] [Start at the teacher targets.] I needed a table whose pooled vectors would make sense to an index full of Stella passages. I began with Stella's encoding of each token. I also gave rare terms more initial weight using inverse document frequency. That gives each row a starting point before I've asked it to represent a whole query. [Follow the two training phases.] For the first pass I saved Stella's vectors and used them as targets. On paired examples I also matched how Stella distributed its scores across candidate passages. A query vector can look close to the teacher's and still put a different passage first. Matching scores gives the table a chance to learn which of those differences matters. Then I trained with query and passage pairs to put the relevant passage ahead of negatives in a frozen bank. I masked known positives and candidates Stella scored close to or better than the positive. Some of those could answer the question too. At export I folded the learned weights into the rows that the encoder reads. The desktop retrain estimate assumes I have the teacher outputs ready. For a new teacher, I estimated eight to 12 hours just to prepare Zero's targets. I can reuse those targets across attempts. Nano can train on Stella's outputs alone, which gives me a way to train a contextual encoder on text without relevance labels.
