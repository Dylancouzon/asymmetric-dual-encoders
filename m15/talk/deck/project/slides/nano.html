Nano’s final optimization

57 h ≈ $95

[~1.75 min] I started Nano with a pretrained bge-small backbone. The projection in the middle takes its features into Stella's 1,024-dimensional space. I trained the backbone and projection together against Stella's vectors for millions of query texts and for document passages. The targets for that fit were the teacher's embeddings, so Nano's optimization needed no relevance labels. Stella still encodes the corpus that Nano searches. The two hardware blocks tell you which work went where. I did Zero's full training and the search for Nano's training recipe on a desktop RTX 3080 with 10 GB of memory. Nano's final optimization ran once on an A100. It took 57.3 hours, which came to about $95 at the recorded rate. That's the bill for the final optimization. Data prep, recipe search, and failed runs add to the cost of building the encoder. I now have two cheaper ways to produce a vector for the same documents.
