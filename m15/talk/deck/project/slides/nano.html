[Draft notes; Sol pass pending.] Nano is a transformer, so the question is how small. We take bge-small, average three of its layers, 12, 8 and 4, and concatenate them into 1,152 features. One linear map takes those into Stella's 1,024 dimensions. Why three layers? Twelve-layer output alone has 384 dimensions, so a linear map can only reach a 384-dimensional slice of Stella's space; three layers give it more room. The head starts from a ridge fit, then backbone and head train together on the same squared error, three query batches for every passage batch. 3.5 million query texts, 834 thousand of them generated by Qwen3-8B in seven query forms; training samples twelve forms evenly from the whole pool. The three-layer head itself was worth plus 0.022 over the last layer alone. The final run was 57 A100 hours, about 95 dollars; the recipe search before it ran on the desktop 3080.
