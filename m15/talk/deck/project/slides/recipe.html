How the Router Decides

Quality: exact-judged NQ queries, Qdrant Edge, oversampling 4 (results/m15_talk_edge_nq.json). Floor and target: the studio defaults. Plan: allocator.py on this laptop's measured encode capacity (results/m15_talk_router_plan.json). Exploratory.

[~2 min] Here's how the router you're about to see makes its choice. Three steps. [01 Measure.] Before any traffic arrives, I score every encoder at every search width on judged queries. That's this menu: the ranking quality I get from each pair. At startup the studio also times each encoder and each search on this machine. [02 Set.] Then I give it two rules, in order. Quality first: the average score of what it serves stays above the floor. Then latency: stay under the p95 target if the machine can. [03 Route.] Every quarter of a second it looks at the traffic and picks the mix of encoders and one search width that gives the best average quality and still fits on this machine. At 1,500 searches a second that's about three quarters Nano, one quarter Zero, at ef 512. This only works because all three search one index. Switching an encoder costs nothing: no second index, no re-encode. To do this on your data you need the same three things: a few hundred judged queries from your traffic, the quality of each encoder and ef on them, and timings on your hardware.
