[Draft notes; Sol pass pending.] Here is the whole method as one optimization. Choose the encoder e, the search breadth ef and the oversampling os that minimize median request time, subject to a relevance floor and a p95 budget that you set before you look. To fill it in: sample and judge real queries; measure each encoder with exact search on your own index, so the graph doesn't hide encoder quality; sweep ef and oversampling for each encoder separately, timing encoding and search together; then pick the cheapest point that clears both constraints. If you serve several budgets, the search settings travel with the encoder choice. The index stays the same. Solved on tonight's NQ index with a 3 millisecond p95 budget: a floor of 0.50 picks Zero at half a millisecond, 0.55 picks Nano at 1.7. Raise the floor to 0.62 and nothing qualifies; only Stella reaches it, at a 35 millisecond p95. So the answer is a budget conversation, on one index.
