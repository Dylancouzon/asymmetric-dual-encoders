Solved on BEIR NQ: 3,452 queries, 2.68M passages, one Stella index in Qdrant Edge, M5 Pro, sequential; nDCG@10 floor, times in ms, ef · os, ef ≥ 64. Exploratory.

[~1.75 min] You need 0.55 nDCG@10 and a 3 millisecond p95. Which encoder ships? [Give the room and Zoom a moment. Point to the 0.55 row.] Nano at ef 64 with fourfold oversampling. It scored 0.554 with a 2.12 millisecond p95 and a 1.74 millisecond median. At a floor of 0.50, Zero at ef 512 and fourfold oversampling gets the job at 0.49 milliseconds median and 0.96 p95. Raise the floor to 0.62 with the same budget and every tested configuration falls outside the constraints. Only Stella clears that relevance floor. It needs about 34.6 milliseconds at p95, so a 35 millisecond budget would let it in. I'd run this on my own traffic. Sample queries and judge the results. Set the relevance floor and the p95 budget before looking at the sweep. Run each encoder with exact search against the existing index to establish its ranking quality. Then sweep search breadth and oversampling for each encoder and time encoding plus search on each request. From the configurations that clear both constraints, ship the one with the lowest median. If you have several budgets, carry ef and oversampling with each encoder choice. The measurements here come from one exploratory laptop run. The decision you'll make comes from your queries and the time your users can wait.
