Zero Needs a Wider Search, and Still Trails

Qdrant Edge, HNSW m=16, ef_construct=100, binary quantization with fp16 rescoring, one build, M5 Pro. Across 25 models in the paper, lookup tables needed a median ef of 256 to match their teacher at 64. Exploratory.

[~2.5 min] Why it matters: the search settings are part of the encoder choice. - Two kinds of HNSW settings in Qdrant: - Set when the index is built: `m`, the links per passage (16, the default), and `ef_construct`, the candidates checked when choosing links (100). Change them and you rebuild. - Set per request: `hnsw_ef`, the candidates kept during the search walk. Wider finds more true neighbours and takes longer. - Oversampling, with quantization: 1-bit binary codes in RAM, full vectors on disk. Fetch 4 times the results with the codes, then rescore with the full vectors. - [Curves] Each line: of the encoder's own exact top 10, how much does the search find? - [Circles] Zero at ef 1024 still finds less than Stella at ef 32. - Why: the graph's links were built from Stella's vectors. Zero's query vectors land a little off, so the walk needs more room. Across 25 models in the paper, tables needed ef 256 to match their teacher at 64. - So: same graph, but each encoder gets its own ef and oversampling per request. Next: the encoder and the search width are one decision, and the best answer changes with traffic. So I let a router decide.
