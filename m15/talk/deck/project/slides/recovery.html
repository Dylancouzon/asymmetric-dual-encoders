[~1.75 min] HNSW links document vectors to other document vectors. I leave all those links in place when I change the query encoder. The new vector changes which documents the search scores highly as it walks. To see what that walk costs me, I get an exact top ten for each encoder and check how many of its own neighbors the graph finds. [Start at Stella at ef 32. Follow across to Zero at ef 1,024.] Hold oversampling at four for this plot. On NQ, Zero searched at the farthest setting we tested and still recovered less of its own exact top ten than Stella did at the first setting. In the paper's 25-model study, I started from each teacher's recovery at ef 64. The median lookup table needed ef 256 for the same recovery on FiQA and TREC-COVID. On SCIDOCS it needed 128. I got a clue before even fitting a table. Take the teacher's queries and divide their nearest-document distance by the typical distance between neighboring documents. When the queries sat farther from the documents by that measure, tables tended to lose more recovery. The rank correlation with the signed gap was minus 0.64. That's a count of neighbors. If the question is how much judged relevance I lose, use nDCG. On TREC-COVID, only 12 of 25 models needed a wider search to match their teacher's nDCG loss.
