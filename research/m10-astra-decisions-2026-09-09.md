**DECISION 1: Re-point to A4 versus A3 at 20M, reusing the completed F-bge-small arm if configuration parity is documented.** Train only A3, with F-bge-small’s seed, batch, initialization and complete 20M three-cycle schedule; compare their final checkpoints descriptively on authorized development surfaces. The registry and exposure table identify F-bge-small as an existing 20M full-corpus arm, so a second A4 run may be unnecessary. Before launching, document identical training configuration except corpus, including sampling and targets. The additional files I would need for that verification are `m10src/corpus_loader.py` and `m10src/trainer10.py`, alongside the recorded F-bge-small run configuration. **If parity cannot be documented, cut SYNTH-20M; do not silently expand it into two new 20M runs.** This costs 20M new examples, approximately **8.9 box-hours and zero cloud dollars**, conditional on parity. It buys a dose-transfer observation for the consequential corpus choice. It trades away a test of the eventual build batch and establishes neither 200M transfer nor seed robustness. E proceeds independently; this diagnostic changes no selection.

**DECISION 2: Yes—run ANCHOR seed 1 at 5M on the box.** Freeze everything except the training seed, retain the existing seed regardless of outcome, and report both absolute scores, the signed difference and its family decomposition. Cost: **5M examples, approximately 2.2 box-hours, zero cloud dollars**. The reporting value warrants that cost, but call it an anchor sensitivity observation, not a seed-variance estimate that “calibrates ten contrasts.” Alternatives can move with the anchor across seeds; their paired effect variability remains unknown. The sentence you may write is: **“At 5M examples, the second training seed changed ANCHOR’s COV macro by Δ = [seed 1 minus original]; this single between-run difference describes anchor sensitivity, while the screen’s query-resampling intervals condition on the trained checkpoints and exclude training-seed variability.”** Do not claim reproducibility, bounded seed noise, seed-adjusted significance, or confirmed verdicts—even if Δ is nearly zero.

**What we are not seeing**

1. **The missing cheap baseline is a frozen bge-small backbone with only an alignment head trained into stella’s space.** The deployment requirement is compatibility with one document index. That does not itself require updating 33M backbone parameters. Freeze bge-small, cache its three pooled layers, and fit the same 1152→1024 linear head against the existing teacher targets. This could preserve much of the retrieval backbone’s existing coverage while making training substantially cheaper; it offers no backbone inference saving. Neither success nor failure is established by the listed screen arms. **What settles it:** a development-only comparison of this frozen-backbone alignment model with the fully distilled anchor, using the same training corpus and evaluation surfaces. If competitive, the project has a cheaper training path. If clearly worse, it has direct evidence that backbone adaptation earns its cost.

2. **“Form-balanced” makes the taxonomy itself an untested training policy.** Seven new labels receive 58.3% of query presentations because there are seven labels. Splitting one semantic category into two could increase its training weight without adding a single query. That is a deeper issue than the already-disclosed A4–A3 exposure confound: the recipe’s allocation is sensitive to how someone names categories. At 200M examples under the stated allocation, each form receives 12.5M presentations—approximately **201 presentations per available health text, 218 per argument text, and nine per factoid text**, on average. These are repetition counts, not evidence of overfitting, but “40× dose” is far from “40× coverage.” **What settles it:** compare allocation policies on the *same* A4 corpus, with weights attached to explicit semantic groups or an intended workload rather than the number of labels. Register that comparison separately; retain today’s recipe selection.

3. **A release success can still leave the original failure unresolved.** Improving scientific/biomedical averages and repairing the weak CQADupStack components are distinct accomplishments. Merely adding another aggregate development surface will not make them equivalent. The missing reporting commitment is to identify, before the final result, what counts as recovery on **each original weak component**, using teacher-normalized retention and absolute retrieval quality together. This need not become a retroactive release veto. **What settles it:** the already-registered family-A development diagnostics on both CQADupStack components, followed by the authorized final component-level results. If the release bars pass while those components remain weak, ship under the registered rule—but describe the outcome as a stronger shared-index recipe, not demonstrated repair of M9’s coverage failure.

4. **The screen optimized component selection, while the important uncertainty is whether additional training changes the answer.** “Nothing moved” is the wrong scorecard: the screen supplied evidence for corpus inclusion and backbone choice, and supported the prescribed head. But it cannot establish that the retained defaults are optimal at build dose. Nor does failure to resolve establish that its alternatives have no useful effect. For the same approximately 52 box-hours, I would have prioritized **A3/A4 × two training seeds × complete 5M/20M schedules**: 100M examples, approximately **44.5 hours**, leaving several hours for the frozen-backbone baseline. That trades broad architecture and optimizer exploration for matched evidence about corpus benefit, seed sensitivity and dose interaction. **What settles it now:** the reused 20M comparison from Decision 1 supplies one piece cheaply. A future matched-seed corpus comparison supplies the next; another isolated anchor seed alone would not.
