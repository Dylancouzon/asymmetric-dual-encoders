## BLOCKER

- `instructions-m10.md:299–302, 318–325, 211–213, 270`

  Quote: “**read by no M10 decision**” and “Comparators only, **no M10 candidate**, so no arm is favoured.”

  Finding: This is false as written. The resolution check scores `bge-small` and `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, the exact two family-F student candidates. Its result determines whether family F runs; if it cuts the screen, F defaults to MiniLM. It also makes a COV-based M10 decision before COV selects any arm, contradicting the stated untouched-selector condition. The statistic is mathematically interpretable as \(\hat\Delta-L_{0.025/16}\), paired/bootstrap-stratified within component, but one comparator pair’s interval width cannot prove that an MDE-sized contrast for every future arm is unresolvable.

  Fix: Run the precision check on a pre-registered calibration subset excluded from the eventual COV macro, using two frozen models that are not M10 arms/candidates. Lock the exact metric, component weights, score artifact hashes, formula, and a rule that exposes only the precision verdict—not model direction. Otherwise explicitly classify COV as already used for protocol design and stop calling it untouched.

- `m10/PLANNING.md:72–75, 116–130`; `instructions-m10.md:154`

  Quote: “hosted open-weights inference … if the smoke-measured pass would exceed 50 GPU-hours” and “**expected** … **$400–670**.”

  Finding: The permitted hosted-generation branch is not included in the budget table. The table prices GPU generation at $30–100, whereas §5 prices the fallback at $110–330. Replacing the GPU-generation line yields roughly $459–885 before optional seed/extension spend, not $400–670. At the high end, the advertised optional seed and extension allocations cannot fit beneath $1,000.

  Fix: Add mutually exclusive GPU-generation and hosted-generation scenarios to §6, including all provider, input/output-token, disk, and egress costs. Make the lock reserve mandatory completion costs first, then seed 1 and a fixed extension allocation from the actual remaining dollar balance.

## MAJOR

- `instructions-m10.md:225–234`

  Quote: “**D-NCE**, L2 + λ·InfoNCE at temperature τ on synthetic query-role examples only…”

  Finding: D-NCE is not fully executable without further choices. It does not define its logits or reduction: whether the denominator is the positive plus 128 negatives, the exact normalized vectors/template for the seed passage, and how the NCE term is weighted across synthetic versus non-synthetic examples. The τ rule is calibrated on a teacher softmax over 128 negative candidates, while NCE has a 129-item positive-plus-negative set.

  Fix: Lock the literal loss equation, including unit normalization, raw/document template, positive plus all 128-negative denominator, mean reduction, λ application, and handling of unavailable/removed seed passages. State explicitly that τ selected by the 128-negative teacher-support rule is reused unchanged for the 129-logit NCE objective.

- `instructions-m10.md:245–248`; `m10/LEDGER.md:9–17`; `m10/STATUS.md:73–75`

  Quote: “repeated while it does; the cap is the build’s remaining GPU-hours … written into the lock as an integer example count.”

  Finding: The extension rule is not yet a mechanical pre-registration. “Improved the best COV macro” does not identify the comparison baseline; if “best” includes the current endpoint, the gain is zero. It also does not say whether the cap permits partial cycles, what LR schedule a partial cycle uses, which accrued/committed costs are subtracted, or how dollar ceiling is converted into “GPU-hours.” The ledger skeleton does not require these fields.

  Fix: Lock a function such as “endpoint \(k\) minus the best endpoint strictly before \(k\), using full-precision fixed COV macro,” exact evaluation hashes, complete-cycle versus partial-cycle policy, partial-cycle schedule, measured-rate conversion, billed-spend source, mandatory-cost reserve, and a maximum integer extension-example budget. Add a hard stop that cannot launch a cycle exceeding the reserved dollar balance.

- `m10/PLANNING.md:72–75`; `instructions-m10.md:156–160, 167–170`

  Quote: “3.0M queries × (~300 prompt + ~60 output) … ≈ 180M [generated tokens]” and “`max_new_tokens` … 60, **or 400** for the argument and conversational forms.”

  Finding: The generation budget assumes 60 output tokens for all 3M queries, despite two 250K-query forms allowing 400 tokens and the argument form requiring 120–220 words. At maximums, generated output is 350M tokens, not 180M; at 1,500 generated tok/s this alone is about 65 GPU-hours before prompt-prefill work. Generated-token throughput also does not budget the 900M prompt tokens, retries, JSON failures, or batching overhead.

  Fix: Budget from smoke-measured, per-form end-to-end request throughput and observed output lengths, including prefill, retries, and overhead. Apply the 50-hour fallback gate to the full projected generation job, not generated tokens alone.

- `instructions-m10.md:359–365`; `m10/PLANNING.md:83–88, 94–100`

  Quote: “The first screen arm … measure[s] the real rates” and “the first screen arm’s measured rate replaces it.”

  Finding: The 560 ex/s, 600 docs/s, and 1,500–3,000 generated tok/s figures are reasonable provisional planning inputs, not validated budgets. Measuring only the first screen arm is insufficient protection: it occurs after expensive generation/encoding work, may be A1 rather than the representative 75/25 anchor, and cannot establish the 50/50 document-heavy or MiniLM rates. The quoted 46 confirmation hours also understates its own assumptions: \(85M/(560×3600)=42.2\) training hours, plus 16 confirmation-run evaluations at the stated ~0.3 hours is about 47.0 hours.

  Fix: Before scale-up, benchmark generation end-to-end, stella encoding, and representative 75/25, 50/50, bge, and MiniLM training mixes; record the actual provider price. Recompute a spend-capped stage schedule before generation and again before the remaining arms. Use ~47 hours for the stated worst-case confirmation model, or revise the evaluation assumption.
