Two kinds of number live on this page, and they are not interchangeable.

The BEIR-15 table is descriptive: alpha = 0, no gate, no threshold, no superiority or equivalence claim. It does not enter or reweight the registered six-set inference. The reserved-four contrasts below it are the registered inference — R1 and R2, their weightings fixed in advance, their intervals from a paired within-dataset bootstrap.

Intervals quantify query resampling only. They exclude training-seed variation. An interval that straddles zero is an unresolved superiority test, never a finding of equivalence.

Descriptive · aggregate

Totals across the benchmark

Unweighted mean nDCG@10 per system — the BEIR convention — taken two ways: across all fifteen datasets, and across the eleven with no disclosed teacher exposure. Both are shown because they do not agree on the ordering.

mean of all 15 mean of the 11 clean
“Clean” means no disclosed teacher overlap, the registered sense of the word; the four excluded are fiqa, arguana, fever and climate-fever. It does not mean clean of comparator exposure: msmarco, nq, hotpotqa and quora are labelled comparator-training and stay in the clean eleven, so a comparison against bge-small or leaf-ir-asym over this subset still includes datasets those comparators were trained on. Each mean weights every dataset equally, so a dataset with 49 queries counts as much as one with 10,000 — the BEIR convention, and a weighting choice like any other.

The two totals disagree, and the disagreement is the point. nano-dense sits behind bge-small on all fifteen (0.5081 against 0.5171) and ahead on the clean eleven (0.5137 against 0.5059). bge-small averages 0.5479 across the four disclosed-exposure datasets against nano's 0.4929 — a gap of +0.0550. FEVER is where that gap lives: its per-dataset gap is +0.2431, contributing +0.0608 to the four-row mean, while the other three disclosed datasets contribute −0.0058 between them.

This does not resolve R1. R1 is the registered contrast, computed on the reserved four with weights fixed in advance, and it remains unresolved. A mean over a different set of datasets under a different weighting is a separate descriptive quantity — it is not a second opinion on the registered test, and reading it as one would be exactly the re-weighting-after-the-fact the registration exists to prevent.

What the registered contrasts were computed from

The four datasets behind R1 and R2. FEVER sits outside NDO-3 and outside both contrasts, and is shown for completeness rather than as evidence.

FEVER carries disclosed exposure for both stella and the comparators, which is why it is quarantined. Adversarial review examined its reversals — nano roughly 0.24 below both comparators, bge-small above stella-query there — and found them compatible with the disclosed exposure but declined to attribute them to it. A pattern consistent with contamination is not a measurement of contamination.
Reading notes

What these numbers do and do not support

  • R1 is unresolved, not equal. NDO-3 gives +0.0032 with an interval straddling zero, but the per-dataset deltas disagree in sign — +0.0187 on DBpedia, +0.0013 on android, −0.0260 on english — and the query-pooled weighting turns it −0.0121 with an interval that excludes zero. Report the weightings together.
  • R2 is consistent. Nano is behind leaf-ir-asym by −0.0389 on NDO-3, and behind under every reweighting, across all three clean datasets.
  • Fusion's value tracks BM25's strength on the dataset. DBSF@100 lifts zero-dense +0.089 to the top of hotpotqa, where BM25 scores 0.585; it costs nano-dense 0.036 on MS MARCO, where BM25 scores 0.226. M12's DBSF@100 recommendation is a disclosed product-policy override with no equivalence interval, so this dependence belongs reported beside it.
  • The bge-small > stella-query reversal is not unique to FEVER. It also appears on hotpotqa and climate-fever, neither of which carries FEVER's disclosed double exposure. That weakens — without refuting — reading the FEVER reversal as a contamination signature.
  • Only quality numbers are here. These come from exact search. Qdrant and Edge measurements establish deployment behaviour and latency; ANN recall is never mixed into this comparison.
  • This page carries comparator per-dataset absolutes. Those remain unpublished on model cards unless the owner rules otherwise (2026-09-15); the whitepaper carries the full comparator table. Treat this as internal evidence, not card content.
Provenance

How the run got here

  • Three attempts, no wasted work. A projection gate refused a viable run by extrapolating documents-per-second from the longest 1.1% of the corpus; a host crash cost ~9 h and one shard; a latent split bug pointed MS MARCO at a split name its qrels repository does not publish. Each is written up in m20/FINDINGS.md, every attempt receipt preserved, and the original deadline inherited throughout rather than reminted.
  • Verified against prior references. SciFact reproduces the recorded smoke values for all eight systems (bm25 0.6790938361 against a recorded 0.679093836); NFCorpus reproduces M12's registered DBSF@100 value 0.344206137000484. On MS MARCO, bge-small's 0.4082 matches the official model card's 0.40833 — both render as 0.408 — while bm25's 0.2256 is close to the published 0.228 without matching it, a 0.0024 difference consistent with bm25s tokenising and stemming differently from Anserini.
  • Every shard re-hashed after the crash. 1,978 shards, 131.1 GiB — one damaged, and its re-encode reproduced the pre-crash hash byte for byte.
