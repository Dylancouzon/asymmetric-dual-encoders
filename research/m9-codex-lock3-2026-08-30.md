## B1. Closed-form warm start

**BLOCKER — the warm-start choice and λ are DEV-tuned despite the ledger saying otherwise.**

The ledger says λ was selected on training residual at [LEDGER.md:270](/home/dylan/asymetric-dual-encoders/m9/LEDGER.md:270). The actual probe evaluates every λ on SCREEN-3 and selects `max(screen3_macro)` at [head_probe.py:84](/home/dylan/asymetric-dual-encoders/m9src/head_probe.py:84) and [head_probe.py:97](/home/dylan/asymetric-dual-encoders/m9src/head_probe.py:97). The artifact itself admits this is optimistic at [m9_head_probe.json:71](/home/dylan/asymetric-dual-encoders/results/m9_head_probe.json:71).

Selecting on training residual would not rescue it: ridge training residual is expected to favor the smallest λ, so it merely chooses the arbitrary bottom of the grid. The solve also minimizes unnormalized output error, while the actual training objective normalizes the head output first.

Concrete fix: quarantine the current anchor as diagnostic. Choose λ with a training-only split—e.g. 50K ridge fit, 10K validation—using the actual normalized L2 objective, with a locked grid and tie rule; then refit on 60K and rerun the anchor under a regenerated lock.

**MAJOR — “identical treatment, so no contrast moves” is false.**

The same nominal algorithm has factor-dependent effects:

- The λ and warm-start decision were tuned only for incumbent stella × bge × raw prompt, potentially privileging the incumbent, anchor student and baseline prompt.
- Each teacher receives its own supervised 60K-target fit, so the teacher screen now measures teacher-index quality **plus linear decodability after an oracle head fit**.
- The prompt arm refits the head in each prompt’s feature space, so it is an end-to-end prompt-plus-refit contrast, not a pure prompt contrast.
- The mix arm is warm-started only on query targets, not its 70/30 query/document distribution. It tests “add documents after a query-only initialization,” not independently optimized query-only versus mixed recipes.

Concrete fix: define the estimand explicitly as “per-arm training-only-calibrated ridge initialization plus SGD.” Apply the same calibration algorithm independently to each arm; include 70/30 targets in the mix arm’s calibration. Alternatively use one fixed common query initialization and narrow the claims accordingly.

**MAJOR — it adds uncounted dose.**

Fitting on the same training pool is not, by itself, DEV leakage; those examples are legitimate training inputs. But it is an additional supervised phase: 60,000 frozen-backbone forwards plus access to 60,000 teacher targets and a solve. None of those tokens or seconds enter the registered dose or throughput.

This makes the anchor’s retention-vs-dose curve incomparable to LEAF’s random-head recipe and invalidates “same dose” language for `m9s1` versus `m9s1c`.

Concrete fix: report ridge-fit examples, tokens, teacher-target accesses, backbone FLOPs and wall time as Stage 0 dose. Compare both fixed-SGD-dose and fixed-total-compute curves.

**MAJOR — `m9s1c` is useful but does not fully price the warm start.**

It is the right same-seed, same-SGD-budget ablation for the final effect of the selected initialization. It is not compute-matched, λ-selection-honest, or variance-aware. One seed cannot separate initialization value from dropout/order interaction.

Concrete fix: retain it as an ablation, rename its estimand “fixed-SGD-dose warm-start delta,” and add either matched-total-wall-clock checkpoints or at least two additional random-head seeds. Do not call it the price by itself.

## B2. Adequacy gate

**MAJOR — both numbers are arbitrary.**

Retention 0.60 means a final macro of only:

\[
0.60 \times 0.67238 = 0.403428.
\]

Nothing in the power artifact or historical curves relates 0.4034 to correct ranking of teacher/student/prompt/mix arms. An arm can be flat, pass at 0.4035, and remain far too weak for a 0.0056–0.010 contrast to represent final-system ranking.

Likewise, 0.02 is not tied to uncertainty, remaining headroom, or a rank-reversal rate. It is 0.02 over approximately 14.88M tokens, but code treats it as an unnormalized two-point difference.

**MAJOR — the slope does not measure convergence.**

Checkpoint 3 occurs with LR about `2.40e-5`; checkpoint 4 reaches `1e-5`. A small late gain can therefore mean “the cosine schedule shut learning down,” not “the representation converged.” Also:

- Any negative slope passes, including substantial collapse.
- Only two endpoints are read.
- It checks absolute anchor quality, not stability of any challenger–anchor contrast.
- A good 90%-retention arm gaining 0.0201 fails, while a 60%-retention arm gaining 0.0199 passes.

Concrete fix: replace it with a preregistered continuation of the same anchor at a nonvanishing/token-indexed LR. Measure paired per-query improvement per million tokens, require a small two-sided bound or small improvement relative to remaining headroom, and calibrate the retention threshold against historical checkpoint rank concordance. Until that calibration exists, call it a budget trigger—not a ranking-adequacy gate.

**BLOCKER — gate authorization and ceiling are unguarded.**

`adequacy()` reads `m9_dev_symmetric_stella-400M-v5.json` directly at [screen.py:270](/home/dylan/asymetric-dual-encoders/m9src/screen.py:270). That artifact has no registration block and is absent from the fingerprint. Stage B then trusts any `m9_adequacy.json` containing `"pass": true` without `guard9.eligible()`.

Concrete fix: make the ceiling a numeric registry constant with a pinned artifact hash; validate the anchor, ceiling and adequacy artifact under the same session; recompute the gate inside `require_predecessors` rather than trusting a stored boolean.
